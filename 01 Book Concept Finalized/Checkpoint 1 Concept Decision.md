## 3. What We Reject

The project will **not** use the following as its central historical claim:

> "An ideology tried to kill Gandhi, failed to kill what he represented, and is still trying to destroy his legacy."

The main problems are:

1. **"An ideology" is too abstract.** The evidence supports Godse's individual political and ideological motives within a wider political environment. It does not justify treating an ideology as a single acting organisation.
2. **"What Gandhi represented" is too broad.** Gandhi's image, moral authority, political philosophy, political practice, national symbolism and institutional influence survived to different degrees.
3. **"Still trying to destroy his legacy" is not established as a continuous general claim.** The evidence supports continued contestation, appropriation, rejection, neutralisation, counter-memory and criticism, but not one uninterrupted anti-Gandhi project from 1948 to the present.
4. **Modern criticism is not automatically continuity.** Similarity, political hostility, appropriation or fringe Godse glorification does not prove organisational or ideological transmission from the 1948 assassination network.

## 4. What We Preserve From the Original Idea

The original idea contains a strong historical intuition:

**A political figure can be physically killed without the political meaning associated with that figure disappearing.**

The assassination creates a useful historical hinge because before 30 January 1948 Gandhi was still a living political actor who could speak, intervene, negotiate, revise and redirect his political project. After his death, he could no longer control how others interpreted or used his name, ideas and authority.

The project therefore preserves four connected questions:

1. **Why was Gandhi killed?**
2. **What was Gandhi still trying to accomplish when he died?**
3. **What actually survived when Gandhi survived as a symbol?**
4. **Who subsequently inherited, rejected, appropriated, neutralised or reinterpreted Gandhi?**

## 5. Official Working Concept

> **Gandhi's assassination ended his life as an active political actor, but it did not end the political struggle over what Gandhi meant. After his death, different actors preserved, criticised, appropriated, rejected, neutralised, or reinterpreted different parts of the Gandhi who could no longer speak for himself.**

This is a **working concept**, not a claim that every later conflict derives from the assassination.

## 6. Central Historical Question

> **What happens to political meaning when the political actor who embodied it is permanently removed?**

For this book, the more specific version is:

> **How did Gandhi's assassination transform Gandhi from a living political actor into a contested political inheritance?**

## 7. Core Argument Direction

The book will investigate a sequence:

**living political actor → assassination → loss of political agency → durable posthumous symbol → competing inheritances → contested meaning**

The book will demonstrate this sequence through evidence rather than assuming it.

### 7.1 Gandhi as a living actor

The opening must establish Gandhi's actual final political position, not a retrospective icon. His final months included communal reconciliation, minority security, non-retaliation, India-Pakistan relations, the January 1948 fast, and the unfinished political/social programme visible in the 29 January Lok Sevak Sangh draft.

### 7.2 The assassination

The assassination must be reconstructed through the strongest criminal and primary/near-primary record. Godse's political and ideological reasoning is central, but the book must preserve the distinction between:

- Godse's worldview;
- the broader ideological environment;
- organisations and networks;
- conspiratorial decisions;
- and institutional responsibility.

### 7.3 The loss of political agency

This is a central conceptual hinge. Gandhi's death meant that Gandhi could no longer:

- answer critics;
- revise a position;
- reject an appropriation;
- reinterpret his own legacy;
- or redirect political strategy.

This is the mechanism through which the posthumous Gandhi becomes historically different from the living Gandhi.

### 7.4 The posthumous Gandhi

The book will distinguish at minimum:

- Gandhi's public image;
- national-symbol status;
- moral authority;
- political philosophy;
- political practice;
- institutional influence;
- commemoration and memory.

The evidence strongly supports symbolic survival. It does not support the claim that Gandhi's entire political programme survived intact.

### 7.5 The contested inheritance

The post-1948 field will be analysed through specific mechanisms:

- commemoration;
- criticism;
- rejection;
- reinterpretation;
- appropriation;
- selective use;
- neutralisation where demonstrable;
- counter-memory;
- deliberate discrediting where demonstrable.

These categories must remain distinct.

## 8. Scope of the Book

### Central period

**1947–1948** is the historical foundation and should receive substantial attention because it establishes:

- Gandhi's actual final political project;
- the political environment surrounding the assassination;
- Godse's stated motives;
- the conspiracy record;
- and what exactly was lost when Gandhi died.

### Post-1948 period

The book may extend through later decades, but it should use **selected turning points and case studies**, not attempt to document every controversy involving Gandhi.

### Present day

The present may be used where it demonstrates a documented historical pattern, inheritance, counter-memory, appropriation or new form of contestation.

Modern events must **not** be used retrospectively to establish what 1948 actors intended.

## 9. What the Book Will NOT Claim

The book will not claim, without new evidence specific to the claim:

- that the RSS as an institution ordered or executed Gandhi's assassination;
- that the Hindu Mahasabha as an institution ordered or executed Gandhi's assassination;
- that Savarkar was definitively the mastermind;
- that Godse's exact RSS status in January 1948 is settled;
- that Gandhi's January 1948 fast was solely about the ₹55 crore payment;
- that Gandhi's complete political programme survived intact;
- that every criticism of Gandhi is an attempt to erase him;
- that every political appropriation of Gandhi is hypocrisy;
- that every modern anti-Gandhi action continues the 1948 assassination project;
- that there is one uninterrupted organised campaign to destroy Gandhi's legacy.

## 10. Required Historical Controls

### Actor-specific attribution

Always identify the person, organisation, movement, period and evidence involved.

### No ideology-as-agent shortcut

Do not write as though an abstract ideology itself attended meetings, approved plans or carried out actions.

### No membership-to-responsibility shortcut

Membership or affiliation does not establish organisational knowledge, approval or operational participation.

### No modern-to-1948 shortcut

Current political claims do not retroactively establish historical motive.

### No symbol-to-philosophy shortcut

Gandhi's continued image and fame do not prove continuation of his full political philosophy.

### No criticism-to-destruction shortcut

A serious criticism must be treated as criticism unless evidence shows deliberate discrediting or erasure.

### No continuity-without-transmission

Historical continuity requires evidence of transmission, inheritance, organisational continuity, personnel continuity, textual continuity, stated continuity of objective, or another demonstrable mechanism.

### No false resolution of disputed evidence

Where court records, inquiries, political correspondence or later scholarship differ, the disagreement must remain visible.

## 11. Evidence Standard for the Manuscript

### Load-bearing claims

Use primary or near-primary records wherever reasonably available, supported by serious scholarship.

### Quotations

Verify exact wording against authoritative sources before manuscript use.

### Legal material

Keep criminal judgments, inquiry commissions and later historical interpretations institutionally distinct.

### Godse

Treat his statements as evidence of his stated reasoning, not automatic proof of complete historical causation.

### RSS / Hindu Mahasabha / Savarkar

Use actor-specific, source-specific attribution and preserve unresolved disputes.

### Post-1948 claims

Use case studies with clear documentary evidence and avoid turning heterogeneous later disputes into one unified campaign.

## 12. Working Book Proposition

A concise internal formulation is:

> **The assassination ended Gandhi's political agency, not the political life of Gandhi's meaning. His death created a permanent struggle over which Gandhi would survive.**

This proposition should guide the next checkpoint, but it must still be tested through the actual historical cases chosen for the manuscript.

## 13. Current Confidence

**Overall concept confidence: HIGH for the working direction; MEDIUM for the eventual final thesis wording.**

Why:

- the assassination record is strong;
- Godse's political/ideological reasoning is well supported;
- Gandhi's final political activity is strongly documented;
- symbolic survival and continuing contestation are well supported;
- the stronger continuity/destruction claim is not supported and has been deliberately removed.

The main remaining uncertainty concerns how to select and connect post-1948 cases so that the book demonstrates a genuine historical mechanism rather than merely compiling examples of Gandhi being discussed.

## 14. Remaining Research Before Manuscript Architecture

The next stage should target:

1. the immediate post-assassination transformation of Gandhi's public and political meaning;
2. documented turning points in state and public construction of Gandhi's memory;
3. a limited number of strong case studies of appropriation, rejection, neutralisation and counter-memory;
4. stronger evidence for any specific modern continuity claim that survives initial screening;
5. the relationship between Gandhi's actual final political programme and later simplified versions of Gandhi.

The next checkpoint should **not** return to broad discovery research unless one of these targeted questions exposes a major conceptual problem.
