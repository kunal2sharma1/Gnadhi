## 11. Biggest Risks to the Book's Current Thesis

### 1. The continuity problem

The most vulnerable part of the current concept is the implied line from **1948 assassination ideology → post-1948 struggle → present-day attempt to destroy Gandhi's legacy**. The first and third elements may each be historically demonstrable without proving the connecting line.

### 2. The undefined “what Gandhi represented” problem

The phrase can become unfalsifiable unless it is decomposed into specific political, ethical, religious, social, and symbolic claims.

### 3. The institutional-collapse problem

The concept could accidentally collapse Godse, Savarkar, RSS, Hindu Mahasabha, and broader Hindu nationalist currents into a single actor. The Phase 2 assignments expressly prohibit this. citeresearch2-assignment citeresearch3-assignment

### 4. The presentism problem

Modern political conflicts can tempt the author to read contemporary narratives backwards into 1948. The packets must establish historical continuity rather than infer it from present-day similarity.

### 5. The legacy-as-victory problem

Saying that Gandhi “survived” symbolically does not establish that his political programme survived, prevailed, or remained internally coherent.

### 6. The polemical-language problem

“Destroy his legacy” is substantially stronger than “criticise,” “reinterpret,” “appropriate,” “contest,” or “reduce his public standing.” The book should not use the strongest term unless the evidence supports intention and continuity.

## 12. Strongest New Insights From the Research

Because the substantive Claude packets are absent, no new historical insight can honestly be attributed to their collective research.

However, the research architecture exposes several hypotheses that deserve testing:

### The book may need two histories rather than one

The history of **why Gandhi was killed** and the history of **what happened to Gandhi's memory after his death** should not automatically be treated as one continuous process.

### Continuity should become a research question

Instead of assuming that modern challenges to Gandhi are descendants of the ideology behind the assassination, the project should ask what evidence of transmission, institutional continuity, intellectual inheritance, or recurring political objectives actually exists.

### “Legacy” should be treated as contested rather than singular

Gandhi may have multiple posthumous legacies: moral, political, anti-colonial, religious, social, nationalist, international, institutional, and symbolic. Appropriation and opposition may coexist.

These are hypotheses for later testing, not findings from the absent packets.
