2. It establishes the adversarial tests that Worker 1's actual options must pass when `03.7.1` is restored.

The current evidence produces a clear hierarchy of thesis vulnerability.

The weakest propositions are the ones that make **an abstract ideology the direct actor**, treat **symbolic survival as survival of an intact political programme**, or assert **one continuous anti-Gandhi project from 1948 to the present without documentary continuity evidence**. Those propositions carry major causal, chronological and conceptual risks.

The strongest historical territory is the connection among: **Godse's documented ideological reasoning; the political environment in which he acted; the distinction between individual, network and organisational responsibility; Gandhi's final political activity; and the subsequent long contest over the meaning of Gandhi.** The research supports a sustained argument about **contested meaning / contested inheritance** more securely than it supports a continuous-destruction narrative.

Even that stronger direction needs attack. A serious historian could argue that Gandhi's symbolic afterlife was partly produced by the postcolonial state, Congress and international institutions; that criticism of Gandhi is not itself evidence of a unified field of contestation; that the history of post-1948 Gandhi is too heterogeneous to support one overarching mechanism; and that the assassination's ideological context must not become an all-purpose explanation for later political disputes.

The most defensible conclusion at this stage is therefore not that one thesis has been proven, but that **the book's central historical problem should probably be framed around the transformation and contestation of Gandhi's meaning after his death, while retaining the assassination and Godse's ideological world as the founding historical rupture rather than as proof of a continuous present-day conspiracy.**

No thesis should be frozen until the missing Worker 1 file is restored and each actual option is audited individually.

#### Family A — Ideology caused the assassination

Core idea: Gandhi's assassination is fundamentally a story of ideological hostility, with Godse's politics situated within a wider Hindu-nationalist field.

#### Family B — Assassination failed to erase Gandhi

Core idea: Gandhi was physically killed, but his public/moral/symbolic presence survived and became politically consequential.

#### Family C — The meaning of Gandhi remained contested after 1948

Core idea: the assassination ended Gandhi's life but did not end disputes over what Gandhi meant; different actors preserved, criticised, appropriated, rejected, neutralised or reinterpreted different parts of his legacy.

#### Family D — Gandhi was transformed into a contested national symbol

Core idea: the important historical process was not simply survival but transformation — from living political actor to posthumous national, moral and political symbol whose meaning could be claimed by competing actors.

#### Family E — A continuous ideological effort has sought to destroy Gandhi's legacy

Core idea: the ideology associated with Godse continued after 1948 and remains engaged in an ongoing effort to weaken, erase or destroy Gandhi's legacy.

#### Family F — One specific organisation or actor should bear institutional responsibility

Core idea: the book's central explanatory burden rests on demonstrating that an organisation or senior individual had sufficient knowledge, authorization or operational responsibility for the assassination.

The last family is especially important because the existing Phase 3.5 research deliberately warns against collapsing individual association into institutional responsibility. ## 4. Option-by-Option Historical Audit

### Option family A — Ideology caused the assassination

#### Historical defensibility

This is substantially defensible if "ideology" is treated as a major component of Godse's motive rather than a sufficient or autonomous cause.

The strongest evidence is Godse's own courtroom record, supplemented by the documented political context described in Phase 3.6: Gandhi's communal policy, Partition, the January 1948 fast, the Pakistan payment, and Godse's opposition to Gandhi's moral-political authority. Phase 3.6 classifies this as well supported but explicitly warns against monocausal explanation.

#### Causal validity

This option works only if the causal chain remains visible:

**ideas → grievances/political interpretation → relationships/networks → conspiratorial decisions → act**

The thesis fails if it becomes:

**ideology → assassination**

because that formulation erases the actors and decision points through which ideology became action.

#### Chronological validity

Strong for 1940s history. Weak if it automatically becomes an explanation of later decades.

#### Conceptual precision

"Hindu nationalism," "Hindutva," "Hindu Mahasabha ideology," "RSS ideology," "Hindu Sanghatanism," and "Godse's worldview" cannot be treated as synonyms.

#### Evidence dependence

Good if anchored in Godse's statements and serious scholarship. Moderate risk because the most important primary source is also the assassin's self-presentation.

#### Counterexample resistance

A strong rebuttal is that Godse's worldview may have been highly personal and that other people in the same ideological field did not make the same choice. That does not destroy the claim that ideology mattered; it destroys the stronger claim that the ideology itself acted.

#### Scholarly attack surface

A serious historian could accuse the book of reifying ideology unless it keeps actor-level causation explicit.

#### Reader attack surface

A skeptical reader may reasonably ask: "If the ideology itself caused the murder, why did millions of people exposed to the same ideas not assassinate Gandhi?"

That question is fatal to a simplistic thesis and useful for a more precise one.

### Option family B — Assassination failed to erase Gandhi

#### Historical defensibility

Strong at the level of **public memory and symbolic persistence**. We have durable state commemoration, international recognition and continued public use of Gandhi's name and image.

The proposition becomes weaker when "Gandhi" means his full political philosophy.

#### Causal validity

The thesis must explain the mechanism of survival. Did the assassination itself preserve Gandhi's symbolic authority? Did the shock of martyrdom, postcolonial state-building, Congress memorialisation, education, international recognition, and social movements produce the durability? The current evidence supports the existence of survival better than a single causal mechanism explaining it.

#### Chronological validity

Potentially strong if the book treats 1948 as a turning point rather than the sole cause of all later memory.

#### Conceptual precision

"Survival" must be disaggregated into image, moral authority, political philosophy, practice, institutional influence and symbolic status.

#### Evidence dependence

Moderate. The symbolic survival evidence is strong; the survival of the complete political programme is not.

#### Counterexample resistance

The strongest counterexample is that Gandhi's image remained strong while major elements of his political practice were marginalised. That means the thesis cannot equate survival with victory.

#### Scholarly attack surface

A reviewer could accuse the book of making a martyrdom teleology: Gandhi dies, therefore the meaning of Gandhi becomes permanently stronger. The evidence does not justify that deterministic sequence.

#### Reader attack surface

A skeptical reader may ask whether Gandhi's image survived because it was made safe and ceremonial, rather than because his most demanding political ideas prevailed.

That objection must be addressed directly.

### Option family C — Contested meaning after 1948

#### Historical defensibility

This is currently one of the strongest directions in the evidence base. Phase 3.6 explicitly states that Gandhi's meaning remained contested and identifies criticism, rejection, appropriation, neutralisation, counter-memory, scholarly reassessment and state commemoration.

#### Causal validity

The option does not require a single causal mechanism. This is a strength, but also a danger: a thesis that explains everything may be falsified by nothing.

The book must therefore define a narrower question such as how and why Gandhi's meaning was repeatedly reconstructed by actors with different political objectives.

#### Chronological validity

Better than a continuity thesis because it does not assume that the same actors or institutions persisted unchanged. It still requires periodization.

#### Conceptual precision

"Contestation" must not become a synonym for "anything anyone ever said about Gandhi." Cases should be classified: criticism, rejection, appropriation, neutralisation, counter-memory, commemoration, or inheritance.

#### Evidence dependence

Strong and diversified. It can draw from Gandhi's own writings, state commemoration, political speeches, critics, scholarly works and specific case studies.

#### Counterexample resistance

The strongest counterexample is that a plural and heterogeneous set of arguments may not constitute one coherent historical field. The answer is not to deny the heterogeneity but to make heterogeneity itself part of the subject.

#### Scholarly attack surface

The strongest attack is that "contestation" is too broad and sociologically obvious to sustain a full-length historical argument.

The book avoids this only if it asks **what changed, who changed it, why, and with what consequences**.

#### Reader attack surface

A reader may ask: "What is new here? Everyone knows Gandhi is controversial." The book needs a deeper mechanism than mere controversy.

### Option family D — Transformation of Gandhi into a contested national symbol

#### Historical defensibility

Strong but requires a precise pre-1948 baseline. Gandhi was already an international and Indian political symbol during his lifetime. The assassination therefore transformed an existing symbol; it did not create one from nothing.

#### Causal validity

The book must distinguish:

- Gandhi as living political actor;
- Gandhi as martyr;
- Gandhi as national symbol;
- Gandhi as moral vocabulary;
- Gandhi as political programme.

The historical transformation is strongest in the first three categories.

#### Chronological validity

Potentially strong if the book traces the process through distinct post-1948 phases rather than making a single 1948-to-present jump.

#### Conceptual precision

"Transformation" requires an identifiable before/after comparison.

#### Evidence dependence

Strong if built from speeches, memorial institutions, state documents, textbooks, public rituals and political uses over time.

#### Counterexample resistance

Because Gandhi was already a major symbol before death, a critic could say there was no fundamental transformation, only intensified memorialization.

That is a serious challenge and must be tested rather than assumed away.

#### Scholarly attack surface

The book could be accused of over-centering the assassination when state formation and decolonisation independently produced posthumous canonization.

### Option family E — Continuous destruction of Gandhi's legacy

#### Historical defensibility

This is presently not defensible as a general thesis.

The research identifies:

- substantive criticism from several ideologically unrelated traditions;
- selective appropriation by actors who disagree with parts of Gandhi;
- fringe Godse glorification;
- possible neutralisation of difficult aspects of Gandhi in identifiable cases;
- and durable state commemoration.

These facts do not form a single continuous actor.

#### Causal validity

The thesis requires a causal and intentional chain from 1948 to the present. The research has not established it.

#### Chronological validity

This is the central failure. The thesis jumps from 1948 to the present unless the book proves transmission, inheritance, institutional continuity or repeated strategic reproduction.

#### Conceptual precision

"Destroy" and "legacy" are both dangerously elastic. Does replacing a textbook paragraph count? Public criticism? Statues? Selective commemoration? Rejecting nonviolence? Rejecting Gandhi's economics? Glorifying Godse? The thesis can survive only by defining these terms so tightly that many examples will fall outside it.

#### Evidence dependence

High dependence on inference from scattered modern examples.

#### Counterexample resistance

The strongest counterexample is that many actors who reject major Gandhian ideas continue to invoke Gandhi's image, and the Indian state continues extensive commemoration. This does not prove that no destruction occurs; it disproves a simple generalized erasure model.

#### Scholarly attack surface

Very high: presentism, teleology, collective-agent fallacy, confirmation bias, selective case selection, and political advocacy disguised as continuity history.

#### Reader attack surface

A skeptical reader can ask: "Show me the organisation, the documents, the chain of transmission, the stated objective and the continuity of personnel." At present the book cannot answer that question generally.

### Option family F — Organisational-responsibility thesis

#### Historical defensibility

A narrower institution-specific thesis could be possible, but the current record does not justify broad categorical attribution to the RSS or Hindu Mahasabha.

The strongest available evidence is actor-specific: Godse, Apte, other convicted conspirators, Savarkar's contested relationship, contemporary state correspondence, and the distinction between operational conspiracy and broader political atmosphere.

#### Causal validity

This thesis has a clear testable structure but requires stronger evidence than the current base supplies.

#### Chronological validity

Strong for 1948; irrelevant to the later legacy question unless separately linked.

#### Scholarly attack surface

Very high if the book turns a disputed legal/historical question into a settled accusation.

#### Reader attack surface

A reader can immediately raise the acquittal, the distinction between individual membership and institutional responsibility, and the absence of a clearly established command document.

## 5. Strongest Counterexamples

The following are the most powerful attacks on the thesis families collectively.

### Counterexample 1 — Gandhi's public survival is undeniable

Durable commemoration, memorials, public institutions, anniversaries and international recognition make a total-erasure thesis implausible.

This does **not** prove that every aspect of Gandhi's thought survived. It proves that "destroy Gandhi" cannot simply mean "make Gandhi disappear from public memory."

### Counterexample 2 — Gandhi's philosophy did not survive intact

The opposite mistake is equally serious. A critic can show that post-independence Indian politics did not become a straightforward Gandhian system. Therefore symbolic survival is not equivalent to political survival.

### Counterexample 3 — Gandhi has many independent critics

Ambedkarite/Dalit critiques, Marxist critiques, postcolonial/race critiques and conservative or Hindu-nationalist critiques arose from different premises.

Therefore criticism of Gandhi cannot be used as a proxy for one anti-Gandhi ideology.

### Counterexample 4 — Some opponents also appropriate Gandhi

Hindu-nationalist political actors have at times invoked Gandhi selectively. That is hard to reconcile with a theory that all later opposition equals simple attempted destruction.

### Counterexample 5 — Fringe Godse glorification is not equivalent to mainstream continuity

The research identifies genuine fringe counter-memory, but it is explicitly characterized as marginal. Treating fringe activity as representative of an entire contemporary political field creates a category error.

### Counterexample 6 — Gandhi himself was internally contradictory

A thesis that treats "Gandhi" as a single stable ideological object will have difficulty explaining tensions between his late ideas, earlier positions, caste debates, economics, political methods, religious language and evolving strategies.

### Counterexample 7 — The assassination did not create Gandhi's symbolic status from nothing

Gandhi was already internationally recognized before 1948. Any thesis claiming that the assassination alone transformed him into a symbol overstates causation.

## 6. Major Logical Risks

### 6.1 Abstract-agent fallacy

"An ideology killed Gandhi" gives an abstract set of ideas the agency of concrete people. The book must name people and decisions.

### 6.2 Membership-to-responsibility fallacy

A person's membership in an organization does not establish that the organization knew, approved, directed or facilitated his criminal action.

### 6.3 Conspiracy-to-organisation fallacy

A multi-person conspiracy does not automatically equal organizational conspiracy.

### 6.4 Symbol-to-philosophy fallacy

A surviving symbol does not prove survival of the symbol's complete ideological content.

### 6.5 Criticism-to-destruction fallacy

Criticism, rejection, revision and discrediting are analytically distinct.

### 6.6 Appropriation-to-hypocrisy fallacy

Selective political use of Gandhi does not automatically prove cynical intent.

### 6.7 Analogy-to-continuity fallacy

A modern actor resembling a historical actor is not evidence of historical continuity.

### 6.8 Correlation-to-causation fallacy

The fact that Gandhi became a larger symbol after his death does not prove that assassination itself caused every later form of symbolic authority.

### 6.9 Consensus-from-repetition fallacy

Multiple research packets can repeat a common source. Repetition is not independence.

### 6.10 Martyrdom-teleology

The fact of Gandhi's death can tempt the author into treating every later event as part of a preordained contest over Gandhi. Historical actors did not necessarily see their actions through that later lens.

### 6.11 Presentist continuity

Current disputes may resemble 1948 disputes without being descendants of them.

## 7. Major Historical Risks

### 7.1 Overstating Godse's representativeness

The evidence establishes Godse's location within a political field. It does not make him a statistical or doctrinal representative of everyone in that field.

### 7.2 Overstating RSS responsibility

The organisational record is insufficient for a categorical claim that the RSS directed the assassination. The precise membership question is disputed, and organizational responsibility requires a separate evidentiary showing.

### 7.3 Overstating Savarkar

Savarkar's acquittal is a legal fact; the later Kapur Commission assessment is a separate historical record. Neither should be used to erase the other.

### 7.4 Reducing Gandhi's final fast to ₹55 crore

The primary record shows a broader communal and civic purpose, while government documents show the payment issue was also consequential. The thesis must hold both facts simultaneously.

### 7.5 Treating post-1948 history as a simple Gandhi-versus-one-side binary

The evidence is too heterogeneous. Gandhi had allies, critics, appropriators, opponents, admirers and institutionalizers across ideological camps.

### 7.6 Converting public memory into proof of ideological victory

Gandhi's image survived, but the political practice associated with Gandhi did not simply become India's governing philosophy.

### 7.7 Treating current examples as self-interpreting

A textbook deletion, public memorial, political speech or heritage redevelopment can be evidence of many different processes. Intent must be demonstrated.

## 8. Chronology and Continuity Risks

The book should not use the following chain without filling the evidentiary gaps:

**1948 assassination → 1950s–70s memory → 1980s–2000s political change → 2010s–2020s politics → continuous ideological project**

That sequence is not a continuity argument. It is a chronological sequence.

To establish continuity, each transition requires evidence of transmission or inheritance.

### Required continuity tests

For every later case, ask:

1. Who are the actors?
2. What did they inherit from whom?
3. Is there organizational continuity?
4. Is there personnel continuity?
5. Is there textual continuity?
6. Is there explicit citation or adoption of earlier arguments?
7. Is there continuity of objective?
8. Is there continuity of strategy?
9. Did the actors themselves describe the connection?
10. Could the later development be explained equally well without reference to 1948?

If question 10 can be answered "yes," the thesis should not automatically label the later event as continuity.

### Stronger historical categories than continuity

Depending on the evidence, a later case may be:

- recurrence;
- resemblance;
- reinterpretation;
- appropriation;
- inheritance;
- institutional continuation;
- counter-memory;
- or genuine ideological continuity.

The book should prove which category applies rather than assuming continuity.

## 9. Definition Problems

### Legacy

Must distinguish:

- public image;
- memorial status;
- moral reputation;
- political philosophy;
- political practice;
- institutional inheritance;
- economic ideas;
- communal politics;
- constructive programme.

### Ideology

Must identify whose ideology and the relevant period. A generic use of "Hindu nationalism" risks flattening historically distinct movements.

### Representation

"Godse represented X" can mean influence, membership, rhetorical alignment, organisational authorization, or statistical representativeness. The book must specify which.

### Survival

Survival can mean visibility, influence, institutional practice, political adoption, or moral authority. These must not be merged.

### Destruction

Needs an operational definition. Possible meanings include:

- physical removal;
- institutional abolition;
- delegitimation;
- reputational discrediting;
- curricular removal;
- symbolic replacement;
- political neutralisation.

Each is a different historical claim.

### Contestation

Should mean sustained conflict over meaning, use, interpretation or political inheritance, not merely disagreement.

### Appropriation

Should require a demonstrable actor adopting Gandhi's symbols, language or authority for a political purpose. It does not automatically imply bad faith.

### Continuity

Requires a demonstrable link across time. Temporal succession alone is insufficient.

## 10. Strongest Option — Attempted Destruction

The "attempted destruction" option is the strongest emotionally and the weakest evidentially among the major recoverable thesis families.

It is attractive because it gives the book a clear antagonist and a simple narrative: Gandhi is killed physically but his meaning survives, and the struggle then continues in another form.

That narrative has serious historical problems.

### Attack 1 — Who is "they"?

The pronoun presupposes a continuous collective actor. The present research does not establish one.

Godse, the Hindu Mahasabha, RSS actors, Savarkar, later Hindu-nationalist actors, current political actors, textbook controversies and heritage disputes cannot be grouped merely because some of them oppose aspects of Gandhi.

### Attack 2 — What does "destroy" mean?

If destruction means making Gandhi disappear from public memory, the thesis is contradicted by durable commemoration.

If destruction means reducing the influence of particular Gandhian ideas, the proposition becomes more plausible but must be shown case by case.

If destruction means attacking Gandhi's moral authority, evidence exists in some episodes, but it does not establish one continuous campaign.

If destruction means rejecting Gandhi's politics, then many independent critics qualify, including critics who have nothing to do with Godse's ideological environment.

### Attack 3 — Why assume continuity?

A modern actor may reject nonviolence for reasons arising from contemporary politics, caste debates, economic preferences, nationalism or other concerns. The similarity of criticism does not prove inheritance from 1948.

### Attack 4 — What about appropriation?

The fact that actors who disagree with Gandhi also invoke Gandhi is a major counterexample to the idea of pure destruction. It suggests that Gandhi remains politically useful and symbolically available even to people who reject substantial parts of his programme.

### Attack 5 — What about independent criticism?

Ambedkarite, Marxist, postcolonial and other critiques undermine any theory that all challenges to Gandhi belong to one ideological lineage.

### Attack 6 — What about Gandhi's own complexity?

A simple "Gandhi versus ideology" story risks transforming a historically complex political thinker into a fixed symbolic object whose meaning is assumed in advance.

### Attack 7 — What about the state?

Durable state commemoration complicates a totalising theory of destruction. A more interesting historical question is how states can simultaneously institutionalise Gandhi while depoliticising or selectively presenting him.

That is a more difficult claim — and potentially a better one — because it can be tested against actual institutions and practices.

## 11. Can the Strongest Option Survive?

### Strongest attempted rebuttal in favour of the destruction thesis

The best defence is not that every critic of Gandhi belongs to the same conspiracy. It is narrower:

> Some actors can retain Gandhi's national image while weakening politically disruptive elements of his philosophy, while fringe actors explicitly rehabilitate Godse. The result may function as a form of legacy destruction even if no single organization coordinates it.

This argument is materially stronger than the original claim because it replaces a unified actor with multiple mechanisms.

### Does that rebuttal survive?

**Partly.**

It can support narrow claims of:

- selective neutralisation;
- deliberate counter-memory;
- reputational attacks;
- political narrowing of Gandhi's image.

But it still cannot support the historical claim that these are all manifestations of the same ideology or one continuous campaign unless specific continuity evidence is added.

### Verdict

The broad "attempted destruction" thesis **does not survive as presently formulated**.

A narrower proposition — that Gandhi's posthumous legacy has repeatedly been **contested, appropriated, narrowed, countered and sometimes deliberately attacked** — does survive the current evidence better.

That is not a semantic rescue. It changes the historical object from a single enemy to a process of contested inheritance.

## 12. Stronger Alternative, If Required

Because the exact Worker 1 options are missing from the repository, a replacement is offered only as a **provisional adversarial alternative**, not as a final thesis.

### Proposed alternative

> **Gandhi's assassination ended his life as a political actor, but it did not end the struggle over his political meaning. After 1948, Gandhi became a contested inheritance: governments, movements, critics, admirers and later political actors repeatedly preserved, rejected, appropriated, neutralised, or reinterpreted different parts of what he had represented while arguing over whether his ideas remained politically usable.**

### Why this is stronger

It preserves the book's real intellectual core — the relationship between assassination and legacy — without requiring:

- a single ideology as a historical actor;
- an institutional guilt claim not yet established;
- a continuous chain from Godse to present-day politics;
- the assumption that Gandhi's full philosophy survived intact;
- or the assumption that all later opposition is destruction.

### But it has a vulnerability

It can become too broad. "Everyone fought over Gandhi" is not enough for a book.

To survive, the thesis must select a small number of high-evidence mechanisms or case studies and show **how Gandhi's meaning changed and why those changes mattered**.

The alternative is therefore stronger, but not automatically safe.

## 13. What Survives From the Author's Original Intuition

The original intuition contains several historically valuable ideas, but they are not all equally defensible.

### What survives strongly

**1. Gandhi's assassination was not merely the killing of a private individual.**

The research strongly supports treating it as a political act with ideological content and wider networks.

**2. The assassination did not end Gandhi's historical significance.**

This is strongly established at the level of public memory, moral symbolism and continued political reference.

**3. The meaning of Gandhi remained contested.**

This is the strongest surviving element of the original idea. The post-1948 record is not one of simple preservation or simple erasure.

### What survives with narrower language

**4. "What Gandhi represented" can survive only after being disaggregated.**

The surviving objects include image, moral authority, national symbolism and selected ideas — but not one intact programme.

**5. There really are examples of efforts to weaken particular aspects of Gandhi's legacy.**

But they must be demonstrated as individual cases, not assumed to form one continuous project.

### What does not survive as a general thesis

**6. "They" as a continuous actor.**

Not established.

**7. "Still trying to destroy" as a general description of post-1948 Indian politics.**

Not established.

**8. A simple equivalence between Godse's ideological environment and every later critic or opponent of Gandhi.**

Historically unsafe.

### What is genuinely original and worth preserving

The most valuable idea is not the claim that Gandhi's enemies simply failed to eliminate him. It is the deeper paradox:

> **Killing Gandhi removed the living political actor while creating a posthumous symbol whose meaning could no longer be controlled by Gandhi himself.**

This creates a powerful historical problem: once Gandhi died, different actors could claim, compress, sanitize, reject, weaponize or reinterpret a political legacy whose original author was no longer present to define its boundaries.

That is a stronger historical puzzle than a simple story of assassination followed by repeated attempted destruction.

## 14. Recommended Conceptual Direction

The evidence currently points to a **two-level structure** rather than a single thesis sentence.

### Level 1 — The founding rupture

Gandhi's assassination should be investigated as a political and ideological act involving Godse's individual convictions, his organizational relationships, a network of conspirators, and a wider political environment.

This section must preserve the distinctions among:

- Godse;
- Hindu Mahasabha;
- RSS;
- Savarkar;
- other conspirators;
- ideological influence;
- organizational responsibility.

### Level 2 — The posthumous struggle over meaning

The book then asks how Gandhi's meaning was reconstructed after his death.

This is where the research can test:

- state canonisation;
- public memorialisation;
- criticism;
- selective appropriation;
- neutralisation;
- Godse counter-memory;
- scholarly reassessment;
- political inheritance;
- and, where evidence permits, deliberate discrediting.

### Central conceptual warning

The bridge between Level 1 and Level 2 must **not** be assumed to be continuity of one ideology.

The book should explicitly test whether later phenomena are:

- continuity;
- inheritance;
- recurrence;
- appropriation;
- analogy;
- counter-memory;
- or something else.

The research may eventually discover genuine continuity in some cases. It has not yet proved a universal continuity.

## 16. Handoff to Product Manager

### Most important decision

**Do not freeze the original "continuous destruction" proposition.** The present evidence does not carry its heaviest load-bearing assumptions.

### What the evidence supports most strongly

- Godse's assassination of Gandhi was a political/ideological act.
- Godse operated within a wider political environment without becoming a stand-in for all of it.
- Individual, organisational and ideological responsibility must remain separate.
- Gandhi's public and moral significance survived after his death.
- Gandhi's complete political programme did not simply survive intact.
- Gandhi's meaning remained contested across multiple, ideologically distinct constituencies.

### Five most dangerous thesis moves

1. **"An ideology killed Gandhi."** The evidence supports Godse's ideology as a major motive, not an abstract ideology as an autonomous historical agent.
2. **"The RSS/Hindu Mahasabha killed Gandhi."** Institutional responsibility is not established at the required level.
3. **"What Gandhi represented survived intact."** Symbolic survival is stronger than political-programme survival.
4. **"The same forces are still trying to destroy Gandhi."** Continuity is not demonstrated generally.
5. **"Anyone attacking Gandhi is part of the same historical struggle."** The independent existence of Ambedkarite, Marxist, postcolonial and other criticisms defeats this compression.

### Strongest provisional direction

The evidence currently favors a book about **the afterlife and contested inheritance of Gandhi's political meaning**, with the assassination serving as the founding rupture and Godse's ideological world serving as one necessary part of the explanation — not as a universal key to every later political use of Gandhi.

### Acceptance condition before thesis freeze

The Product Manager should require:

- restoration and audit of the actual Worker 1 thesis-option file;
- option-by-option survival ratings after exact text is available;
- a falsifiability test for the surviving option;
- explicit definitions of legacy, destruction, contestation and continuity;
- a periodized evidence map for post-1948 cases;
- and a clear statement of what evidence would disprove the chosen thesis.

Until those conditions are met, **Checkpoint 1 should remain conceptually open even though the broad research-discovery stage is complete.**
