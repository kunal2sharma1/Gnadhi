## 2. Source Quality Assessment

### Tier 1 — Primary / near-primary

For the six-packet audit, these are the sources that should anchor major factual claims:

- trial and appellate judgments;
- government records and contemporary communiqués;
- Gandhi's own writings, speeches and correspondence;
- original Godse statements and court material;
- original political correspondence;
- original RSS and Hindu Mahasabha records where preserved;
- archival material created close to the events.

### Tier 2 — Strong scholarship

Use for interpretation, historiography and contested context:

- university-press books;
- peer-reviewed scholarship;
- specialist historical monographs;
- major scholarly biographies;
- serious studies of Gandhi, Godse, Savarkar, the RSS, Hindu Mahasabha and post-1948 memory.

### Tier 3 — Reputable journalism / institutional history

Useful for discovery, chronology and corroboration, but should not be the sole foundation of a central disputed claim where a primary or scholarly source is available.

### Tier 4 — Partisan / advocacy

Useful as evidence of what an organization or ideological camp says about itself or its opponents. It should not by itself establish disputed facts about third parties, conspiracy, intent or causation.

### Tier 5 — Weak tertiary

Wikipedia, copied summaries, unattributed quotation sites, reader notes, social-media posts and AI-generated tertiary material should be treated as discovery leads only.

### Key source-control rule

A lower-tier source is not automatically useless; the problem is using it for a proposition that requires stronger evidence. A partisan site reproducing an authentic primary document may be useful for locating that document, but the manuscript should cite the underlying primary record where possible.

### Source independence

Repeated claims are not independent corroboration. Six Claude packets citing the same biography, website or quotation database would still represent one underlying evidentiary chain.

## 4. Quotation Verification Risks

No completed Claude quotation sections are currently available in the specified Phase 2 paths. That prevents a proper quote-by-quote audit.

When recovered, the following categories require high scrutiny:

1. Godse's courtroom statement — verify against the authoritative court record or earliest reliable transcript.
2. Godse statements about RSS membership or departure — verify date, venue, language and whether retrospective.
3. Patel correspondence about RSS, Hindu Mahasabha or the assassination — verify the original letter and date.
4. Kapur Commission quotations — verify against the Commission report itself.
5. Gandhi's January 1948 statements — verify against the Collected Works/authoritative archival text.
6. The 29 January 1948 Congress-related draft — distinguish an actual draft/proposal from an adopted institutional resolution.
7. ₹55 crore quotations — check Gandhi's own statements separately from government records.
8. Ambedkar quotations — verify the original publication/speech/letter rather than quotation websites.
9. Savarkar quotations — verify original language, date and translation.

A quotation should not be marked verified merely because multiple websites repeat the same wording. The audit should record speaker, date, document/venue, earliest located source, authoritative source, original-language status, whether the original was consulted, and whether the wording is exact.

## 5. AI / Evidence Reliability Risks

### 5.1 Packet absence can create false completion confidence

Assignments can exist without their actual research outputs. Structural completeness is not evidence completeness.

### 5.2 Repetition can masquerade as corroboration

Multiple AI outputs may draw from the same underlying source.

### 5.3 Precise dates require provenance

Exact dates should be checked whenever chronology affects legal history, conspiracy structure or causation.

### 5.4 Precise numbers require provenance

Witness counts, exhibit counts, membership numbers and other exact figures need traceable sources.

### 5.5 Legal findings must not become universal historical conclusions

A court's finding answers the legal case before it; it does not automatically answer every broader historical question.

### 5.6 Commission findings are not criminal convictions

A commission of inquiry and a criminal court have different mandates and evidentiary functions.

### 5.7 Causal claims can hide inside chronology

"The fast occurred while the ₹55 crore dispute was active" is different from "the fast was undertaken to force payment."

### 5.8 Modern political labels can be projected backward

1940s organizations, terminology and institutional structures should be reconstructed in their historical context rather than assumed identical to later forms.

### 5.9 Self-description is evidence of belief, not neutral proof

Godse's own explanations are indispensable for understanding his stated motives but should not be treated as a complete neutral account of the conspiracy.

### 5.10 Current intent needs current evidence

A claim that actors are "still trying to destroy Gandhi's legacy" requires actor-specific evidence of conduct, target, mechanism and intent.

## 7. Conceptual Risks to the Book

### Risk A — Godse versus an ideology

Godse's beliefs do not automatically represent every person or organization sharing part of his ideological vocabulary.

### Risk B — Ideology versus organization

Ideological influence does not establish organizational authorization, direction or knowledge.

### Risk C — Criticism versus attempted destruction

A serious critique of Gandhi is not automatically a project to erase Gandhi.

### Risk D — Appropriation versus hypocrisy

Selective invocation of Gandhi must be evaluated actor by actor; hypocrisy requires evidence of relevant contradiction and intent.

### Risk E — Historical continuity

Shared ideas or symbols are insufficient to establish institutional continuity from 1948 to the present.

### Risk F — Gandhi as flawless

The assassination context does not eliminate the need to examine Gandhi's serious shortcomings and criticisms.

### Risk G — Image versus philosophy

Gandhi's continued symbolic presence does not prove continued adherence to all of his political philosophy.

### Risk H — Single-cause assassination model

Ideology may be central without being the only causal factor. The research must separate ideology, networks, political events and individual agency.

### Risk I — Later memory projected backward

Later political narratives are evidence of later memory and political use, not automatic evidence of 1948 knowledge or intent.
