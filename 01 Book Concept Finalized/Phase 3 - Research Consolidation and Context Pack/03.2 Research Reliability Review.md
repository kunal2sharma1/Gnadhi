## 2. Source Quality Assessment

### Repository evidence

The Phase 2 directory is titled `Phase 2 - Seven Claude Research Packets`, and its current tree contains seven research directories. Each directory currently exposes a `Research Assignment.md`, not a completed research packet. The assignment standard confirms that the expected packet must separately identify primary evidence, secondary evidence, quotations, competing interpretations, supported claims, unsupported claims, unresolved questions, gaps, and the GPT handoff.

Therefore the first source-quality finding is procedural:

- **The packet sources cannot currently be graded because the packet outputs are not available in the repository.**
- The existence of an assignment is not evidence that the assigned research was completed.
- A commit named or described as a research/checkpoint completion is not, by itself, evidence that the underlying research artifact exists.
- A later GPT worker must not infer the missing packet contents from assignment wording.

### Tier framework to apply when packets are recovered

| Tier | Source class | Reliability use |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | Original trial judgments, appellate judgments, government records, original correspondence, Gandhi's writings, original Godse statements, archival organizational records, contemporary documents | Required anchor for major factual, chronological and legal claims. |
| **Tier 2** | University-press books, peer-reviewed scholarship, major scholarly biographies, specialist historical monographs | Required for interpretation, historiography and contested historical context. |
| **Tier 3** | Reputable journalism, institutional history projects, university explainers | Useful corroboration and discovery; not sufficient alone for the most consequential disputed claims. |
| **Tier 4** | Partisan publications, advocacy sites, political commentary, movement literature used as historical commentary | Useful for documenting what an actor or movement says; weak as sole proof of disputed external facts. |
| **Tier 5** | Wikipedia, social-media posts, copied summaries, quotation sites, reader notes, unattributed material, AI-generated summaries | Discovery only; never the final evidentiary foundation for a central claim. |

### Important distinction

A Tier 4 source may be a perfectly valid **primary source for the proposition that the organization publicly claimed X**. It is not automatically reliable evidence that X actually happened. Similarly, a Tier 3 article quoting a primary document does not become equivalent to the original document merely because the publication is reputable.

### Source-independence warning

The most important control for the eventual packet audit is **source lineage**. Six Claude packets repeating the same claim do not constitute six independent confirmations if all six derive from one biography, one website, one quotation database, or one modern political account.

Each major claim should therefore be recorded as:

`Claim → exact source → underlying source → source tier → independent corroboration → contrary evidence → confidence.`

## 4. Quotation Verification Risks

The repository currently contains no completed Claude quotation sections to audit. That makes the quotation-control problem particularly important rather than less important.

The following categories must be treated as high risk when the packets are recovered:

1. **Godse's courtroom statement** — verify against the original/authoritative court record or the earliest reliable published transcript. A later English rendering is not automatically the exact wording used in court.
2. **Godse's statements about RSS membership or departure** — verify date, venue, original language and whether the statement was contemporaneous or retrospective.
3. **Statements attributed to Patel concerning the RSS, Hindu Mahasabha or the assassination** — verify the actual correspondence and date before using a quotation as evidence of knowledge or causation.
4. **Kapur Commission quotations** — verify against the Commission report itself, not a later website or book quoting it.
5. **Gandhi's January 1948 statements** — verify against the Collected Works/authoritative archival text, with date and venue.
6. **The 29 January 1948 Congress-related draft/"last will" material** — verify the original document and distinguish a draft or proposal from an adopted institutional resolution.
7. **₹55 crore quotations** — verify the government record and Gandhi's own statements separately. Do not allow a later political summary to substitute for either.
8. **Ambedkar quotations** — verify the original publication, speech, letter or interview. Popular quotation sites are especially unsafe for politically consequential wording.
9. **Savarkar quotations** — verify whether a quotation is contemporary, retrospective, translated, excerpted, or editorially reconstructed.

### Quotation acceptance rule

A quotation should not be marked "verified" merely because the same wording appears on several websites. The audit should identify:

- speaker;
- date;
- venue/document;
- original language where relevant;
- earliest located source;
- authoritative published source;
- whether the original was actually consulted;
- whether the wording is exact;
- whether surrounding context changes its meaning;
- whether the quote should remain excluded until verification.

## 5. Evidence and AI Reliability Risks

### 5.1 Missing packet outputs create false completion confidence

The repository currently has assignment artifacts but not the completed packet artifacts. A workflow can therefore look structurally complete while the evidence layer is absent.

### 5.2 Repeated claims can masquerade as independent corroboration

If multiple Claude sessions were instructed from similar material or independently searched the same high-ranking websites, repetition is not independence.

### 5.3 Precise dates require exact provenance

Exact dates should be checked against primary records where the date affects chronology, causation, legal procedure or political interpretation.

### 5.4 Numbers require an identifiable origin

Witness counts, exhibit counts, amounts, organizational membership figures, numbers of meetings, dates of meetings and other precise statistics should never be accepted without a traceable source.

### 5.5 Legal findings must not be converted into broader historical conclusions

A court's finding addresses the legal case before it. It should not automatically be rewritten as a universal historical finding about an organization, ideology or motive.

### 5.6 Commission findings must not be treated as criminal convictions

A commission of inquiry and a criminal court have different mandates and legal functions. If the Kapur Commission is used, the report must be identified as such and its evidentiary basis explained.

### 5.7 Causal claims can hide inside chronological statements

"Gandhi fasted while the ₹55 crore dispute was active" is a chronological proposition. "Gandhi fasted to force release of ₹55 crore" is a causal proposition. They require different evidence.

### 5.8 Modern organizations cannot be projected backward without documentation

A present-day organization's identity, structure, terminology or political position cannot be assumed to be identical to its 1940s counterpart.

### 5.9 Self-description is evidence of belief, not automatic evidence of fact

Godse's own explanation is essential for understanding his stated motives. It should not automatically be treated as a neutral account of the conspiracy or as proof of organizational responsibility.

### 5.10 Present-day intent requires actor-specific evidence

The phrase "still trying to destroy Gandhi's legacy" implies current intent. It cannot be established by showing that someone criticizes Gandhi, quotes Godse, rejects Gandhian politics, or selectively invokes Gandhi. The evidence must establish an actor, an action, a target, a mechanism and an intention.

## 7. Conceptual Risks to the Book

### Risk A — Godse versus an ideology

The evidence must distinguish Godse's individual ideological position from a universal statement about everyone who shared part of his ideological vocabulary.

### Risk B — Ideology versus organization

An ideological environment can influence an individual without establishing that an organization directed the individual's act. Organizational responsibility requires its own evidence.

### Risk C — Criticism versus attempted destruction

A serious criticism of Gandhi may be hostile, unfair, insightful, ideological, political or mistaken without constituting a deliberate attempt to erase his legacy.

### Risk D — Appropriation versus hypocrisy

Selective invocation of Gandhi may be strategic, sincere, symbolic, contradictory or opportunistic. "Hypocrisy" is an interpretation requiring evidence of a meaningful contradiction and relevant intent.

### Risk E — Historical continuity

A claim of continuity from 1948 to the present requires more than similar beliefs. It should identify institutional, textual, personnel, organizational or strategic links.

### Risk F — Gandhi as flawless victim

The assassination does not suspend the need to examine Gandhi's controversial positions and his serious critics. The book must not turn historical victimhood into retrospective perfection.

### Risk G — Survival of image versus survival of philosophy

The continued presence of Gandhi in public symbolism does not prove continued adherence to his political philosophy. Symbolic memory and substantive ideological continuity must be measured separately.

### Additional Risk H — Treating the assassin's ideology as the only explanatory variable

Motives may include ideology, political events, organizational relationships, personal networks, Partition violence, perceptions of state policy and the assassin's own self-conception. The research should not force a single-cause model.

### Additional Risk I — Treating a contested memory as a contemporaneous fact

Later political narratives about 1948 are evidence of later memory and political use. They cannot automatically be projected backward as evidence of what actors knew or intended in 1948.

## 15. Independent Assessment of the Original Concept

The original concept contains a potentially strong historical investigation, but its wording currently combines several propositions that must be tested separately.

The best-supported area is the historical investigation of **Gandhi's assassination, Godse's stated ideology, the immediate political context, the conspiracy proceedings, and the subsequent construction of memory**. These subjects can be anchored in primary records and serious scholarship.

The weakest evidentiary component is the implied proposition of a **continuous present-day campaign to destroy Gandhi's legacy**. That claim requires identification of actors, actions, mechanisms, chronology and intent. It cannot be inferred from criticism, selective quotation, political disagreement, symbolic appropriation or ideological similarity alone.

The most serious conceptual danger is allowing the assassination to become proof of a thesis that the evidence has not independently established. The book's research process should therefore treat the original concept as a hypothesis to be tested, not a conclusion to be defended.

A second danger is the opposite: allowing an anti-Gandhi critique to be dismissed simply because the subject is Gandhi's assassination. Serious criticism is historically relevant and should be evaluated on evidence and argument rather than on the moral status of the critic.

The research base should consequently preserve three distinctions throughout the next phase:

1. **individual belief versus collective ideology;**
2. **ideological association versus organizational responsibility;**
3. **historical continuity versus modern resemblance.**
