# Phase 4 — Adversarial Concept Testing

## Objective
Attempt to break the working concept before the project moves into serious historical research.

## What this phase must accomplish
Test whether the concept is intellectually coherent, historically responsible, and strong enough to justify a full book. Look for alternative explanations, weak assumptions, ambiguous causal claims, overreach, and places where the book could become advocacy instead of investigation.

## Sub-phases

### Sub-phase 4.1 — Strongest Counterargument
**Assigned to:** Claude

**Work:** Read all completed material from Phases 1–3. Construct the strongest serious argument against the current concept and working thesis. Do not attack a weaker version of the argument. Identify what evidence could genuinely defeat or substantially change the thesis.

**Output:** `04.1 Strongest Counterargument.md`

### Sub-phase 4.2 — Alternative Framings
**Assigned to:** ChatGPT

**Work:** Examine whether the book would be stronger if the central argument were framed differently. Compare the current thesis with alternative interpretations that preserve the author's core interest while reducing unsupported assumptions.

**Output:** `04.2 Alternative Framings.md`

### Sub-phase 4.3 — Concept Revision
**Assigned to:** ChatGPT

**Work:** Review the strongest counterargument and alternative framings. Revise the working concept, central question, and thesis where necessary. Explicitly record what changed and why.

**Output:** `04.3 Revised Working Concept.md`

## Agent instructions
Every agent must read all completed files from earlier phases. The purpose is not to defend the author’s original idea at all costs. The purpose is to discover whether the idea survives serious criticism and to improve it before research begins.

## Completion condition
The concept has survived a serious adversarial test, or the weaknesses have been clearly identified and incorporated into a revised working concept.
