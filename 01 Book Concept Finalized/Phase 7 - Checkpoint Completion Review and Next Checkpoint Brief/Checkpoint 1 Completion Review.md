## 3. Final Concept Position

The project will not use the claim that one ideology continuously tried to destroy Gandhi's legacy from 1948 to the present.

The working direction is instead:

> **Gandhi's assassination ended his political agency, but it did not end the political life of his meaning. His violent death produced an extraordinary political and symbolic crisis through which the new Indian state, political leaders, and the public constructed particular meanings of Gandhi. Because Gandhi died while his political project remained unfinished, later actors inherited a fixed archive without the originating actor being able to revise, defend, reject, or redirect what they made of it. The decades that followed became a contest over which Gandhi would survive.**

This is a provisional working thesis, not a permanent historical conclusion.

## 4. Central Question

> **What happens to political meaning when the political actor who embodied it is permanently removed?**

Gandhi-specific form:

> **How did Gandhi's assassination transform Gandhi from a living political actor into a contested political inheritance?**

## 5. Key Methodological Decision

The book will distinguish between:

**assassination-specific effects**

and

**ordinary posthumous processes**.

Strong assassination-specific effects currently include immediate political shock, extraordinary mourning, state intervention, ritual construction, Gandhi/Godse counter-memory, and the fixed endpoint of Gandhi's unfinished political activity.

Long-term fame, commemoration, education, international recognition, later criticism and many forms of appropriation have broader causes and must be demonstrated case by case.

## 6. What Must Be Preserved in Future Work

The project must preserve:

- the distinction between Godse's stated motives and complete historical causation;
- the distinction between individual affiliation and organisational responsibility;
- the distinction between criticism and deliberate discrediting;
- the distinction between appropriation and continuity;
- the distinction between symbolic Gandhi and substantive Gandhian political practice;
- the distinction between immediate 1948 causation and later historical development.
