# 07.2 Lessons and Unresolved Questions

## 1. Major Lessons

### 1.1 The original intuition contained a stronger question than its literal wording

The useful core was not the claim of one continuous ideology trying to destroy Gandhi. The useful question was what happens to political meaning when a living political actor is physically removed.

### 1.2 Loss of agency is necessary but not distinctive by itself

Death ends a political actor's ability to revise, answer, negotiate and reject later interpretations. That is universal. Gandhi-specific importance comes from the fact that he died while his political project was still active and unfinished, and his assassination triggered an extraordinary immediate political and institutional response.

### 1.3 The assassination has a demonstrable immediate causal zone

The research supports a strong link between assassination, political shock, mourning, ritual, state response and early institutional construction of Gandhi's meaning. The causal claim becomes weaker as the timeline extends farther into later decades.

### 1.4 Gandhi's posthumous survival is not one thing

Image, national symbolism, moral authority, political philosophy, political practice, institutional influence and political memory must be treated separately. Symbolic survival cannot be used as evidence that Gandhi's complete political programme survived intact.

### 1.5 Contestation is broader than inheritance

Not every critic inherits Gandhi. Not every appropriation is sincere or cynical by default. Not every disagreement is continuity. The project should use inheritance only where a demonstrable transmission or claim exists, and use contestation as the wider analytical field.

### 1.6 Modern continuity requires proof

The original intuition about a continuing struggle cannot be carried into modern sections without specific evidence of transmission, organisational continuity, personnel continuity, textual continuity, institutional inheritance, stated continuity of objective, or another demonstrable mechanism.

### 1.7 State canonization is a mechanism, not the whole explanation

The new evidence shows active post-assassination state construction of Gandhi, but state-building and pre-existing Gandhi prestige also independently explain much of his long-term symbolic status.

### 1.8 The 29 January 1948 draft is valuable because it shows incompleteness

The Lok Sevak Sangh material should be used to demonstrate an unfinished political project, not presented as a perfectly settled final doctrine.

## 2. Unresolved Historical Questions

### 2.1 Long-term causal weight of assassination

How much of Gandhi's later political meaning can be specifically attributed to the assassination rather than to ordinary posthumous canonisation, state-building, international prestige, institutions and later political appropriation?

### 2.2 Natural-death counterfactual

Which post-1948 processes are likely to have occurred after either mode of death, and which are specifically tied to violent political murder? The counterfactual is a bounded analytical control, not an alternate historical narrative.

### 2.3 Frozen-final-Gandhi hypothesis

Did Gandhi's inability to revise his final political project materially shape later selective reconstructions of Gandhi? This requires case-level comparison of final-period positions with later claims about what Gandhi supposedly supported.

### 2.4 Pre-1948 versus post-1948 contestation

For selected issues, did the structure, institutional setting or political function of disagreement change after Gandhi's assassination?

### 2.5 State canonization and competing memories

How much did the early state-produced Gandhi determine later public memory, and where did other inheritances successfully diverge from it?

### 2.6 Gandhi/Godse counter-memory

How did memory of Godse develop over time, and when did it remain an assassin-centered counter-memory versus becoming part of broader ideological or political movements?

### 2.7 Specific contemporary continuity cases

Are there any modern cases that satisfy the project's strict continuity test? The current research does not establish one continuous anti-Gandhi campaign.

## 3. Assumptions That Must Not Become Facts

- That the assassination explains all later Gandhi memory.
- That the final 29 January proposal was settled doctrine.
- That state commemoration equals full ideological inheritance.
- That criticism of Gandhi is an attempt to erase him.
- That appropriation is necessarily dishonest.
- That all Hindu-nationalist or anti-Gandhi actors are historically continuous with the 1948 conspirators.
- That Godse's statements provide the complete causal explanation of the assassination.
- That modern political events can retroactively explain 1948 motives.

## 4. What Future Research Must Be Able to Change

The project must remain willing to:

- narrow the thesis;
- change the relative importance of assassination versus canonisation;
- replace weak case studies;
- abandon a modern continuity section if evidence fails;
- revise definitions of inheritance/appropriation/neutralisation;
- or redesign the historical arc if the evidence shows a different mechanism.

## 5. Lessons for Later GPT Work

Every later worker must:

1. read the authoritative checkpoint files first;
2. distinguish evidence from inference;
3. identify the actor and period for every major claim;
4. preserve disagreements instead of forcing resolution;
5. avoid political presentism;
6. avoid broad organisation-level claims from individual evidence;
7. use exact primary-source quotations only after verification;
8. treat modern examples as independent cases unless transmission is documented;
9. identify what could falsify the relevant claim;
10. never turn a working thesis into an established historical fact.
