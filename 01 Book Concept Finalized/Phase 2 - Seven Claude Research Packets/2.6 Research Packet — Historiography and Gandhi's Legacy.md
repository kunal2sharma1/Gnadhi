# Gandhi's Historiography, Legacy, Memory, and Competing Interpretations — Research Packet

**Prepared by:** Claude Researcher 6
**Project:** *The Final Walk of Glory*

> **Method note:** The assignment combines historiography and post-1948 legacy because only six Claude research accounts were available. The packet uses scholarly and institutional material plus contemporary reporting. Where primary verification was not possible, that limitation is flagged. The central task is to test whether conflict over Gandhi's meaning continued after 1948 and in what form.

## 1. Research Questions

1. How has Gandhi's image, reputation, political meaning, and legacy been contested, defended, criticised, appropriated, or transformed after 1948?
2. Where do serious historians and scholars agree and disagree about Gandhi, his assassination, its meaning, and his continuing significance?

The governing test is evidentiary: does the record demonstrate an ongoing struggle over Gandhi's meaning, and if so, what specific form did that struggle take?

## 2. Key Findings

- Gandhi's legacy remained contested after 1948 along multiple independent lines: Hindu-nationalist criticism, Ambedkarite/Dalit criticism, postcolonial criticism of his race/empire record, and left/Marxist criticism of religion and nationalism.
- This is not one single anti-Gandhi project.
- Gandhi's image has also been appropriated by political actors who disagree with substantial parts of his philosophy. The evidence supports selective and strategic use in some cases, but not the blanket claim that every invocation is cynical or hypocritical.
- Fringe Hindu Mahasabha activity glorifying Godse is the clearest documented case of deliberate counter-memory aimed at rehabilitating Gandhi's assassin. It is real but marginal and should not be treated as representative of all mainstream politics or all Hindu nationalism.
- Selective representation or neutralisation of Gandhi is more common and more defensible as a category than a general "destruction" narrative. Swachh Bharat's emphasis on cleanliness and some heritage/redevelopment disputes illustrate this possibility, while textbook disputes remain mixed and cannot be treated as conclusively intentional whitewashing without further evidence.
- Formal state commemoration of Gandhi has remained extraordinarily durable and broadly bipartisan: Gandhi Jayanti, Martyrs' Day, memorials, national currency imagery, public institutions, and international commemoration.
- Serious historians disagree over the role of religion in Gandhi's nationalism, caste, violence/nonviolence, organisational complicity in the assassination, and the meaning of his legacy.
- The phrase "attempts to destroy Gandhi's legacy" is defensible only for narrow, identifiable examples. It is not well supported as a general description of Indian politics, education, or culture after 1948.

## 3. Gandhi's Public Memory After 1948

Gandhi's death generated enormous public mourning and durable official commemoration. Gandhi Jayanti and Martyrs' Day became institutional rituals across governments of different political orientations. His national symbolism is unusually stable even where the interpretation attached to that symbolism is contested.

This matters directly to the book's concept: a general erasure narrative is contradicted by the strength and continuity of state commemoration. The conflict is better described as a contest over **what Gandhi means**, not whether Gandhi exists in public memory at all.

## 4. Political Appropriation

### Congress

Congress has consistently claimed Gandhi as an ideological ancestor, though the actual record is more complicated than a simple continuity claim. The party's political practice and later strategic choices mean that symbolic inheritance and substantive fidelity should be analysed separately.

### BJP and broader Hindu-nationalist politics

A major example is Swachh Bharat, launched on Gandhi Jayanti and using Gandhi's spectacles as a symbol. Other political rhetoric has invoked Gandhi's cleanliness, self-reliance, austerity, and swadeshi-related themes.

The evidence supports **selective appropriation** more safely than a categorical claim of hypocrisy. Actors can sincerely identify with some parts of Gandhi while rejecting others.

This is important for the book because it provides a direct counterexample to the idea that post-1948 political struggle around Gandhi is simply an attempt to erase him.

## 5. Criticism and Rejection

### Ambedkarite/Dalit criticism

Ambedkar's critique of Gandhi's caste politics is one of the most important post- and pre-1948 strands of criticism. It is entirely independent of Hindu-nationalist opposition and therefore demonstrates that criticism of Gandhi cannot be treated as one ideological project.

### Hindu-nationalist criticism

This lineage focuses on Gandhi's Hindu-Muslim politics, Partition, the January 1948 fast, and the Pakistan payment. It is historically important because it formed part of the ideological justification used by Gandhi's assassin, but it must remain separate from other criticisms of Gandhi.

### Postcolonial/race criticism

Scholarship on Gandhi's South African period has challenged idealised global representations of Gandhi, especially his racial hierarchy and loyalism toward empire in that period.

### Left/Marxist criticism

Perry Anderson and related scholarship criticise the religious language of nationalism, caste, and the limits of Gandhi's political framework. These criticisms are substantive historiographical arguments, not attempts to erase Gandhi.

## 6. Deliberate Discrediting

The strongest clear example identified is recurring fringe Hindu Mahasabha activity glorifying Godse: shrines/busts, martyrdom observances, public rehabilitation of Godse, and other attempts to recast him as a patriot or martyr.

This is evidence of a **specific counter-memory** aimed at displacing Gandhi's moral narrative with a positive memory of his assassin.

However:

- it is marginal rather than mainstream;
- mainstream political leaders have publicly disavowed Godse glorification at various points;
- the evidence does not justify extending fringe activity to every Hindu nationalist actor or organisation.

### NCERT textbook controversy

The 2023 textbook deletions are relevant but unresolved. Critics argued that material on Hindu extremist hostility toward Gandhi and the RSS ban was removed as part of historical whitewashing. NCERT described the changes as part of a broader rationalisation process that also affected unrelated politically sensitive material.

The responsible conclusion is **mixed evidence**, not settled proof of a Gandhi-specific erasure campaign.

### Gandhi-associated institutions / heritage

The Sarva Seva Sangh and Sabarmati Ashram redevelopment disputes are better classified as heritage/neutralisation questions than as established anti-Gandhi campaigns. Government actors stated restoration/development goals; Gandhian critics argued that such projects could strip institutions of their original political and ethical character. The historical meaning of those disputes remains contestable.

## 7. Neutralisation and Selective Representation

A stronger concept than "destruction" in some cases is **neutralisation**: retaining Gandhi as a safe symbol while reducing emphasis on politically disruptive parts of his thought.

Swachh Bharat is a useful case study because Gandhi is invoked through cleanliness symbolism while more difficult components of his politics — civil disobedience, economic self-denial, Hindu-Muslim unity, decentralisation, and resistance to state power — are not central to that campaign.

This should be presented as a documented narrowing of emphasis, not as automatic proof of bad faith.

## 8. Historiography of the Assassination

Major interpretive differences include:

- the relative importance of Godse's ideology versus immediate Partition-era grievance;
- whether Godse's courtroom statement should be read primarily as sincere, strategic, or a mixture;
- the exact status of Godse's RSS relationship in 1948;
- the weight to assign to the Kapur Commission compared with the original criminal proceedings;
- the extent of Savarkar's personal responsibility;
- whether the assassination is best understood through political history, ideological history, psycho-political interpretation, or a combination.

The packet identifies a spectrum including Ramachandra Guha, Vinayak Chaturvedi, A.G. Noorani, Koenraad Elst, Dhirendra K. Jha, and Ashis Nandy, each emphasising different evidence and explanatory frameworks.

## 9. Historiography of Gandhi's Legacy

Scholarly disagreement remains active on:

- Gandhi's caste politics;
- the relationship between religion and nationalism in Gandhi's thought;
- the limits and effectiveness of nonviolence;
- Gandhi's handling of communal violence;
- the degree to which his political philosophy survived as practice after independence;
- the transformation of Gandhi into a national and global symbol;
- the role of political actors in appropriating or neutralising that symbol.

This is evidence of a **living historiographical field**, not evidence by itself of a coordinated attempt to erase Gandhi.

## 10. What the Evidence Safely Supports

- Gandhi's legacy remained strongly present after 1948.
- Gandhi's meaning continued to be contested by multiple, ideologically distinct groups.
- Political appropriation of Gandhi's symbolism is real and documented.
- Selective representation/neutralisation is a defensible analytical category in some cases.
- Fringe Godse glorification is a real and repeated counter-memory phenomenon.
- Serious scholarly disagreement about Gandhi remains active.
- State commemoration provides strong evidence against a generalised erasure narrative.

## 11. What the Evidence Does NOT Safely Support

- That the BJP or Indian public life generally has undertaken a coordinated project to destroy Gandhi's legacy.
- That all Gandhi criticism is part of the same ideological campaign.
- That Ambedkarite, Marxist, postcolonial, and Hindu-nationalist critiques are one unified tradition.
- That political appropriation automatically proves hypocrisy.
- That the 2023 NCERT changes definitively prove a Gandhi-specific whitewashing campaign.
- That fringe Godse glorification represents all Hindu nationalism or the contemporary BJP/RSS.
- That modern conflicts over Gandhi automatically descend from the ideology behind the 1948 assassination.

## 12. The Most Important Conceptual Finding

The evidence suggests that **"contested inheritance"** may be a stronger and more historically defensible concept than "continuous destruction."

Gandhi's death did not end conflict over his meaning. Instead, different actors have:

- preserved him;
- criticised him;
- appropriated him;
- neutralised parts of his political programme;
- rejected him;
- defended him;
- glorified his assassin;
- or reinterpreted him for new political purposes.

This is a more complicated story than a simple Gandhi-versus-one-ideology narrative, but it may also be a stronger book because it can accommodate contradictory evidence.
