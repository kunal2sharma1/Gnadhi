# Ideological and Organisational Context — Research Packet

**Prepared by:** Claude Researcher 3
**Project:** *The Final Walk of Glory*

> **Method note:** Web-searched contemporaneous records, court/commission material as reported in serious secondary sources, and named historical scholarship. Not original archival research. Exact quotations require primary verification. Present-day political/legal developments are provisional and should be re-checked before publication.

## 1. Research Question

What ideological and organisational relationships are historically documented among the political actors relevant to Gandhi's assassination, and what can responsibly be said about their influence, affiliation, proximity, or involvement?

## 2. Scope

Focus on Nathuram Godse, V.D. Savarkar, Hindu Mahasabha, RSS, relevant associates, the 1948–49 trial, Kapur Commission, and serious historiography. Modern political disputes are included only where they directly illuminate how this history is interpreted today.

## 3. Key Findings

- Godse was an active Hindu Mahasabha member and newspaper editor around the time of the murder.
- Godse's RSS status in January 1948 remains genuinely disputed. The RSS's institutional position is that he had left years earlier; Godse's own statements, his brother Gopal's later remarks, and later investigative research complicate that account.
- Savarkar was tried as an alleged mastermind and acquitted. The later Kapur Commission reached a more adverse historical conclusion about Savarkar's circle using additional testimony not available at the original trial. That was not a criminal conviction.
- The RSS was banned in February 1948 and leaders were arrested, but no institutional conspiracy charge against the RSS as an entity was established. The 1948 ban should not be confused with a court finding that the organisation ordered the assassination.
- Sardar Patel's 1948 correspondence is especially useful for preserving an individual-versus-institution distinction: he described an "extreme section" of the Hindu Mahasabha as involved in the conspiracy and described RSS activity as contributing to a dangerous atmosphere rather than equating it with a centrally planned assassination.
- RSS and Hindu Mahasabha were structurally distinct organisations. They shared ideological ground but differed in organisational strategy and political role.

## 4. Major Individuals

**Nathuram Godse:** assassin; Hindu Mahasabha activist and editor; earlier RSS association; exact RSS status in 1948 disputed.

**Narayan Apte:** operational accomplice; Hindu Mahasabha activist; executed with Godse.

**V.D. Savarkar:** major Hindu Mahasabha leader and ideological figure; Godse's important personal/intellectual influence; tried and acquitted; later Commission evidence created continuing historical controversy.

**M.S. Golwalkar:** RSS chief; denied institutional involvement; arrested after the assassination and later released.

**Digambar Badge:** approver whose testimony was pivotal to the case against Savarkar but was not independently corroborated enough for conviction at the original trial.

## 5. Organisations

### RSS

Founded in 1925. Presented itself as a cultural/character-building organisation rather than an electoral political party. Its historical relationship with Gandhi, Godse, and the Hindu Mahasabha must be reconstructed without anachronistically equating the 1940s organisation with its later descendants.

### Hindu Mahasabha

An explicit political organisation advocating Hindu Rashtra and sharply opposing Congress's composite nationalism and Gandhi's approach to Hindu-Muslim relations. Under Savarkar it articulated a two-nations conception distinct from Congress's nationalism.

## 6. Relationships and Affiliations

- Godse–Hindu Mahasabha: undisputed and operationally significant.
- Godse–RSS: disputed as to exact duration/end date.
- Godse–Savarkar: close personal relationship documented over many years; repeated meetings in January 1948 are reported in trial/Kapur material.
- RSS–Hindu Mahasabha: distinct organisations with ideological overlap and some personnel overlap, especially in Maharashtra.

## 7. Critical Analytical Distinctions

The following must never be collapsed:

1. Individual belief
2. Ideological influence
3. Organisational membership
4. Organisational association
5. Political sympathy
6. Organisational policy
7. Institutional involvement in an event

A statement such as "Godse was influenced by Hindutva" does not establish "Hindutva ordered the assassination." Likewise, "Godse was associated with X" does not establish "X ordered or directed the assassination."

## 8. Ideological Similarities and Differences

Shared ground included opposition to Congress's approach to Muslim political demands, criticism of Gandhi's Hindu-Muslim policy, and versions of Hindu-nationalist nationalism. Important differences concerned organisational structure, political strategy, electoral participation, and the relationship between cultural mobilisation and direct party politics.

A recurring historiographical danger is to treat all "Hindu nationalist" actors as one coherent bloc. The evidence does not justify that simplification.

## 9. Organisational Responsibility

No court or commission established the RSS as an institutional planner of the assassination. Individual members and associates were tried. The Hindu Mahasabha had members involved, while Savarkar's personal role remained contested. The responsible formulation is therefore actor-specific rather than organisation-wide.

## 10. Historiographical Disputes

Major disputes include:

- whether Godse remained connected to RSS in 1948;
- how much weight the Kapur Commission should carry relative to the 1949 legal acquittal;
- whether Savarkar's relationship to Godse should be read as influence, foreknowledge, encouragement, or operational direction;
- how to interpret RSS's "cultural not political" description;
- how much organisational overlap existed between RSS and Hindu Mahasabha in Maharashtra.

## 11. Modern Relevance

The Godse/RSS question remains politically and legally contested in India. Recent courtroom testimony in August 2026 has again raised the disputed question of Godse's RSS status. Because this is a live dispute, modern claims should be re-verified close to publication and should not be used as if they retroactively settle the 1948 historical record.

## 12. Evidence Safely Supports

- Godse was an active Hindu Mahasabha figure at the time of the assassination.
- Godse had a real historical relationship with RSS, but his exact status in 1948 is disputed.
- Godse and Savarkar had a documented close relationship.
- Savarkar's legal acquittal stands, while the later Kapur Commission created a serious historical counter-assessment.
- No institutional RSS command responsibility is established by the record reviewed in this packet.
- Ideological influence, membership, and organisational responsibility are separate propositions.

## 13. What Evidence Does NOT Safely Support

- "The RSS killed Gandhi" as an institutional fact.
- "The Hindu Mahasabha as a whole ordered the assassination" as an established institutional finding.
- Treating Godse's ideology as identical to all Hindu nationalism.
- Treating present-day organisations as historically identical to their 1940s predecessors.
- Treating modern political testimony as a replacement for 1948 primary records.
