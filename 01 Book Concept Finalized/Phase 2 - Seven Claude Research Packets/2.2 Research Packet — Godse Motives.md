# Godse's Motives and Ideological Position — Research Packet

**Prepared by:** Claude Researcher 2
**Project:** *The Final Walk of Glory*

> **Method note:** Web-based research using reported primary trial material, government-commission material, peer-reviewed scholarship, serious journalism, and explicitly flagged advocacy sources. This is a secondary-source synthesis rather than original archival research. Contested claims are identified rather than resolved. Exact quotations require verification against authoritative editions before publication.

## 1. Research Question

What did Nathuram Godse say motivated Gandhi's assassination, what evidence supports or challenges those explanations, and how do serious historians interpret his ideological position?

## 2. Scope and Exclusions

Focus: Godse's stated motives, political and ideological positions, evidence bearing on those motives, relevant affiliations, and competing historical interpretations. The packet does not attempt a full biography of Godse, a complete history of Hindu nationalism/RSS/Hindu Mahasabha, or a persuasive political argument. It keeps Godse's personal ideology separate from claims about whole movements and separates association from organisational responsibility.

## 3. Key Findings

- Godse left a substantial primary-source record: a March 1948 deposition/autobiographical statement and a longer courtroom statement read during the trial and again before the Punjab High Court in May 1949.
- His stated motives combined long-standing Hindu-nationalist convictions with immediate grievances concerning Partition, Gandhi's perceived Muslim "appeasement," and the January 1948 fast.
- His political activity before the assassination included early RSS involvement, active Hindu Mahasabha work, newspaper editorship, and the Hindu Rashtra Dal.
- Whether he remained formally connected to the RSS in January 1948 is genuinely disputed. Older accounts say he left earlier; later investigative research and family testimony challenge the chronology.
- The Kapur Commission later reached a finding concerning Savarkar's circle using evidence unavailable to the original trial, while the RSS as an institution was not criminally found responsible.
- Godse's claim that the final decision was solely his is difficult to reconcile with the documented multi-person conspiracy and prior failed attempt.
- Historians differ on how to weight ideology, immediate political grievance, psychology, and the strategic character of Godse's courtroom self-presentation.

## 4. Godse's Own Stated Motives

In his final courtroom statement Godse described a Brahmin upbringing, pride in Hindu religion and history, reading of nationalist and other political writers, and close study of Savarkar and Gandhi. He framed his core position in Hindu Sanghatanist terms and argued that serving Hindu interests was, in his understanding, serving India.

He attacked Gandhi's near-absolute nonviolence as unrealistic and argued force could be morally required. He cited Hindu epic and historical warrior figures as examples. He described a long accumulation of grievances, with Gandhi's January 1948 fast as the culminating provocation.

He accused Gandhi of exercising excessive moral authority over Congress and the national movement, criticised his use of Hindustani, blamed him and Nehru for Partition, and portrayed Gandhi's January 1948 interventions as favouring Muslims and Pakistan. He said there was no adequate legal mechanism to hold Gandhi politically accountable and presented his own act as a political intervention rather than personal revenge.

These statements are evidence of **what Godse said and believed**. They are not automatically proof that all of his factual claims were correct.

## 5. Political and Ideological Position

Godse's political trajectory is broadly documented:

- early RSS involvement around the early 1930s;
- later Hindu Mahasabha activism and Pune leadership;
- newspaper work through *Agrani/Hindu Rashtra*;
- founding of the Hindu Rashtra Dal in 1942;
- close relationship with V.D. Savarkar.

His recurring themes included Hindu majoritarian nationalism, opposition to what he regarded as Muslim appeasement, criticism of Congress, rejection of absolute nonviolence, support for Hindu consolidation, and preference for Hindi over Hindustani. His anti-caste activism complicates simplistic portrayals of him as merely an orthodox traditionalist.

## 6. Evidence Supporting Parts of His Explanation

The events he cited were real: Partition involved catastrophic communal violence; Gandhi undertook a January 1948 fast; the Government of India released the withheld Rs. 55 crore payment to Pakistan during that period; and Gandhi's positions on communal protection and India-Pakistan relations were politically controversial.

The broader political criticisms he voiced were not invented after the assassination; opposition to Gandhi's handling of communal politics existed in contemporary political circles.

This establishes that his grievances had a genuine historical context. It does **not** establish that his conclusions about Gandhi or his decision to kill Gandhi were justified or factually correct.

## 7. Evidence Challenging or Complicating His Explanation

- The assassination was part of a multi-person conspiracy, which complicates his claim of acting wholly alone in the final decision.
- His RSS-membership account is disputed and may have contained strategic elements; later family testimony and investigative scholarship challenge the simple "left years earlier" narrative.
- His denial of Savarkar's direction is in tension with the documented closeness of their relationship and meetings shortly before the assassination.
- His presentation of the January fast as essentially a financial or anti-Hindu intervention is contested by evidence showing communal peace and protection of Delhi's Muslims as central stated purposes.
- His ideological framing may understate personal, group, network, and immediate political factors.

## 8. Organisations and Relationships

**RSS:** early membership/association is not seriously disputed; exact status in 1948 is disputed. This must remain separate from the question of institutional responsibility.

**Hindu Mahasabha:** Godse's membership and active role before the murder are strongly documented.

**Savarkar:** Godse regarded him as an important intellectual/political influence and personal mentor. Savarkar's own criminal responsibility remains historically contested between his acquittal and later Kapur Commission findings.

## 9. Historiographical Interpretations

Important explanatory approaches include:

- **Ashis Nandy:** a psycho-political reading that treats the assassination as a deeper conflict involving Gandhi's religious and moral challenge to his opponents.
- **Ramachandra Guha:** a narrative-historical interpretation emphasising Godse's opposition to Gandhi's nonviolence and treatment of Muslims within the Partition crisis.
- **Vinayak Chaturvedi:** careful archival analysis of Savarkar's thought and its relation to the assassination, with explicit caution against claims not supported by direct evidence.
- **A.G. Noorani:** a strongly argued case concerning Savarkar, based heavily on trial and Commission records, but openly polemical.
- **Koenraad Elst:** sympathetic to engaging Godse's grievances while still describing the assassination itself as extremist; contested in reception.
- **Dhirendra K. Jha:** investigative research challenging the conventional account of Godse's RSS separation.

The major unresolved interpretive questions are the relative weight of ideology, immediate political grievance, psychology, and strategic self-presentation; whether Godse's courtroom account was wholly sincere; and how far his ideology can be treated as representative of broader political currents.

## 10. What the Evidence Safely Supports

- Godse's assassination of Gandhi was an ideologically and politically motivated act rather than a random or purely personal crime.
- Godse had a substantial history in Hindu-nationalist political activity.
- His Hindu Mahasabha role immediately before the assassination is well documented.
- His relationship with Savarkar was significant and documented.
- His exact RSS status in 1948 remains disputed.
- Individual ideology and organisational responsibility are separate historical questions.
- Godse's own statement must be treated as primary evidence of his reasoning, not as an automatically complete or objective account of causation.

## 11. What the Evidence Does NOT Safely Support

- That Godse represented every Hindu nationalist or the entirety of Hindu nationalism.
- That RSS membership/association proves RSS institutional responsibility.
- That Savarkar's acquittal settles every historical question about his knowledge or influence.
- That Godse's stated grievances were objectively correct simply because the underlying events occurred.
- That ideology alone explains the entire assassination.

## 12. Important Disputes and Gaps

Key unresolved matters include Godse's exact RSS status in 1948, Savarkar's actual level of foreknowledge, the completeness of Marathi-language and archival records, the exact organisational circumstances around the Hindu Rashtra Dal, and whether other government files relevant to the case remain unavailable.

The full Kapur Commission, Marathi primary material, and authoritative editions of Godse's statements should be consulted before load-bearing claims enter the manuscript.
