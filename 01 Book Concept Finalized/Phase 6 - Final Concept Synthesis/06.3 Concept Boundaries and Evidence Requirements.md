# Phase 6.3 — Concept Boundaries and Evidence Requirements

## 1. Scope Boundary

The book is not a complete biography of Gandhi and not a complete history of the assassination conspiracy.

Its scope is defined by one problem:

> **How did the violent removal of Gandhi as a living political actor affect the production and contestation of his political meaning afterward?**

The book therefore uses 1947–1948 as its essential foundation and extends into later decades through selected, evidence-rich turning points and case studies.

## 2. Required Historical Layers

Every major section should distinguish among:

1. Gandhi as a living political actor.
2. The assassination and conspiracy.
3. Immediate mourning and state response.
4. Construction of the posthumous Gandhi.
5. Substantive Gandhian inheritance.
6. Political appropriation.
7. Criticism and rejection.
8. Neutralisation where evidence demonstrates the mechanism.
9. Counter-memory.
10. Modern continuity only where transmission is documented.

## 3. Key Definitions

### Political agency
The capacity of a living political actor to speak, intervene, negotiate, revise, correct, redirect followers, and reject or accept interpretations of his own actions.

### Posthumous political meaning
The political significance assigned to Gandhi by institutions, movements, critics, political actors, scholars, and publics after his death.

### Inheritance
A demonstrable later relationship to Gandhi through explicit authority claims, transmission, institutional continuity, textual use, adoption of identifiable practices, education, or another documented mechanism.

### Appropriation
Use of Gandhi's name, image, language, authority, or selected ideas for a later political or moral project. Appropriation must not automatically be treated as hypocrisy or bad faith.

### Rejection
An explicit refusal of Gandhi's ideas, methods, authority, or political model. Rejection is not equivalent to destruction.

### Reinterpretation
A later reading of Gandhi that assigns different meanings to his motives, ideas, politics, religion, economics, caste position, nationalism, or methods.

### Neutralisation
A process in which Gandhi remains publicly acceptable or celebrated while difficult, disruptive, or politically demanding parts of his thought are reduced, omitted, domesticated, or detached from their original implications. Demonstrate selection and effect; do not infer intent without evidence.

### Counter-memory
A documented memory practice that elevates an alternative figure, narrative, or interpretation against an established Gandhi-centered memory. Godse-related memory is a key candidate but must be demonstrated case by case.

### Continuity
A historical relationship requiring evidence of transmission, organisational continuity, personnel continuity, textual transmission, institutional inheritance, explicit self-identification, stated continuity of objective, or another documented mechanism.

## 4. Claims That Require Strong Evidence

### Assassination
Use criminal records, primary statements, official records, and serious scholarship.

### Godse's motives
Use his own statements as evidence of stated reasoning, while separately evaluating broader causation.

### RSS / Hindu Mahasabha / Savarkar
Use actor-specific and institution-specific evidence. Never infer organisational responsibility from individual association alone.

### Gandhi's final political position
Use Gandhi's own writings, speeches, letters, the January 1948 fast record, government records, and the 29 January Lok Sevak Sangh material.

### Immediate post-assassination transformation
Prioritise contemporaneous broadcasts, Cabinet/Government records, funeral and mourning records, speeches, correspondence, and serious memory scholarship.

### Long-term inheritance
Use case studies with documentary evidence demonstrating the actual mechanism of transmission or contestation.

## 5. Claims That Need Case-by-Case Proof

- Gandhi was appropriated by a particular political actor.
- A particular institution neutralised difficult parts of Gandhi's thought.
- A particular movement inherited Gandhian practice.
- A particular later group engaged in Godse counter-memory.
- A contemporary actor represents continuity with a 1948 actor or organisation.
- A particular educational or commemorative practice materially changed Gandhi's meaning.

## 6. Claims That Should Not Be Made Without New Evidence

- One ideology alone caused the assassination.
- The RSS as an institution ordered or carried out the assassination.
- The Hindu Mahasabha as an institution ordered or carried out the assassination.
- Savarkar was definitively the mastermind.
- Gandhi's entire political programme survived intact.
- Every critic is trying to erase Gandhi.
- Every appropriation is cynical.
- Every modern anti-Gandhi activity continues the 1948 conspiracy.
- There is one uninterrupted anti-Gandhi campaign from 1948 to the present.
- Assassination alone caused Gandhi's entire posthumous legacy.

## 7. Case-Study Admission Standard

A later case should enter the manuscript only if it can answer:

1. Who is constructing or contesting Gandhi?
2. Which Gandhi is being invoked — period, text, idea, symbol, institution, or image?
3. Through what mechanism is the interpretation transmitted?
4. What competing version is displaced, rejected, or challenged?
5. What political, institutional, intellectual, or cultural consequence follows?
6. What primary evidence supports the case?
7. What serious counterinterpretation must be included?

## 8. Counterfactual Rule

The natural-death counterfactual is used only to separate:

### Likely posthumous effects of Gandhi's death in general

from:

### Effects plausibly specific to violent political assassination.

It must never become a speculative alternate-history narrative.

## 9. Chronology Rule

Do not jump from 1948 to the present merely because a modern controversy resembles an earlier conflict.

Every bridge across time requires evidence of the mechanism connecting the periods.

## 10. Source Hierarchy

### Tier 1
Primary sources, government records, original correspondence, Gandhi's own writings, criminal judgments, original organisational records, contemporaneous reporting.

### Tier 2
University-press scholarship, peer-reviewed research, serious academic biographies, major historical studies.

### Tier 3
Reputable journalism and institutional history used mainly for corroboration or discovery.

Lower-quality sources may not carry a load-bearing claim alone.

## 11. Quotation Rule

Every important quotation must be checked against an authoritative source. Record speaker, date, source, page/section where available, and translation status.

## 12. Legal-Historical Rule

Keep distinct:

- criminal convictions and acquittals;
- inquiry/commission findings;
- political correspondence;
- later scholarly interpretations.

None should silently substitute for another.

## 13. Editorial Balance Rule

The manuscript must permit serious criticism of Gandhi, including criticism concerning caste, Ambedkar, South Africa where relevant, communal politics, political methods, fasting, nonviolence, economics, contradictions, and political failure.

This is not cosmetic balance. These criticisms determine what “legacy” actually means.

## 14. Anti-Presentism Rule

Modern political evidence may be used to study modern memory and later contestation.

It may not be used automatically to establish:

- what 1948 actors intended;
- what Godse's wider organisational network intended;
- or that a present organisation is continuous with a 1948 organisation.

## 15. Core Unresolved Questions

1. Which later case studies best demonstrate a real change in Gandhi's political meaning rather than ordinary historical disagreement?
2. How far did immediate 1948 state canonisation determine later public representations?
3. When did substantive Gandhian inheritance diverge from symbolic Gandhi?
4. Which later cases genuinely demonstrate neutralisation rather than simple selective memory?
5. Which modern cases, if any, pass the strict continuity test?
6. Does the evidence ultimately show assassination as a long-term causal force, or primarily as the decisive trigger for an immediate transformation later shaped by other mechanisms?

## 16. Concept Status

**Working concept approved for manuscript architecture.**

The concept is strong enough to proceed, but final emphasis may change after the selected case studies are deeply researched.
