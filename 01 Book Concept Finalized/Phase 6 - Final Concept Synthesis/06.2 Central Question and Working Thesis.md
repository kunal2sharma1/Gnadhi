# Phase 6.2 — Central Question and Working Thesis Finalisation

## 1. Central Historical Question

> **What happens to political meaning when the political actor who embodied it is permanently removed?**

## 2. Gandhi-Specific Question

> **How did Gandhi's assassination change the conditions under which Gandhi's political meaning was produced, remembered, inherited, rejected, and contested?**

## 3. Working Thesis

> **Gandhi's assassination ended his political agency, but it did not end the political life of his meaning. His violent death produced an extraordinary immediate political and symbolic crisis through which the new Indian state, political leaders, and the public constructed particular meanings of Gandhi. Because Gandhi died while his political project remained unfinished, later actors inherited a fixed archive without the originating actor being able to revise, defend, reject, or redirect what they made of it. The decades that followed therefore became a contest over which Gandhi would survive: the moral figure, the national symbol, the political philosopher, the practical reformer, the selective political resource, and the object of counter-memory.**

## 4. What the Thesis DOES Claim

The thesis claims that:

1. Gandhi remained politically active immediately before his assassination.
2. His death permanently ended his direct political agency.
3. The violent nature and timing of his death produced distinctive immediate political and symbolic consequences.
4. The new state and public actively interpreted and institutionalised Gandhi's meaning in the immediate aftermath.
5. Gandhi died while his political project was unfinished.
6. Later actors could select, transmit, appropriate, reject, reinterpret, neutralise, or contest different elements of Gandhi's legacy.
7. The post-1948 Gandhi therefore cannot be treated as a single fixed political object.
8. The long-term history must be explained through specific documented mechanisms and case studies rather than one continuous anti-Gandhi narrative.

## 5. What the Thesis DOES NOT Claim

It does not claim that:

- assassination created Gandhi's entire legacy;
- Gandhi would have become politically irrelevant without the assassination;
- every later interpretation descends directly from the 1948 killing;
- every critic is engaged in erasure;
- every appropriation is insincere;
- every modern anti-Gandhi action continues the 1948 conspiracy;
- one ideology or organisation controlled the entire post-1948 history of Gandhi.

## 6. Strongest Supporting Reasoning

### 6.1 Living Gandhi

Gandhi's final weeks show a continuing political actor rather than a completed historical icon. His fast, communal work, India-Pakistan interventions, and 29 January Lok Sevak Sangh proposal demonstrate ongoing political agency.

### 6.2 Assassination-specific consequences

The immediate response to the assassination included extraordinary national mourning, Nehru's public reframing of Gandhi's role, Cabinet and Government resolutions, political and institutional actions, funeral rituals, and a distinctive Gandhi/Godse memory relationship.

### 6.3 Frozen unfinished project

The 29 January proposal shows that Gandhi had not completed his post-independence political programme. Death fixed the record before he could revise or operationalise it.

### 6.4 Long-term plural legacy

The historical record supports continued symbolic survival, selective political use, substantive Gandhian movements, serious criticism, counter-memory, and reinterpretation. These are not one process but a field of competing relationships with Gandhi.

## 7. Assumptions That Must Remain Visible

1. The assassination-specific effects are strongest for the immediate 1948 period; the farther the book moves into later decades, the more independent evidence is required.
2. The natural-death counterfactual cannot be directly proven and should be used only as a bounded analytical control.
3. Gandhi's unfinished final project is historically important, but no single final Gandhi doctrine should be invented from the 29 January draft.
4. State canonisation is both an effect of the assassination context and an independent nation-building process.
5. Later inheritance must be demonstrated case by case.

## 8. Claims That Could Still Change the Thesis

The thesis would require major revision if research showed any of the following:

- the assassination had little or no distinctive effect on the immediate political construction of Gandhi;
- Gandhi's final political project was not meaningfully unfinished;
- later actors overwhelmingly inherited a stable, uncontested Gandhi;
- the major post-1948 mechanisms can be fully explained without the assassination-specific 1948 events;
- the proposed case studies cannot demonstrate meaningful differences between symbolic survival and substantive political inheritance.

## 9. One-Sentence Working Thesis

> **Gandhi was physically killed in 1948, but what followed was a struggle over who could define what remained of a political leader who could no longer define himself.**

## 10. Internal Editorial Rule

Whenever the manuscript makes a strong causal statement, ask:

**Is this an assassination effect, a broader consequence of Gandhi's death, a nation-building effect, an ordinary posthumous process, or a later independent development?**

If the category cannot be established from evidence, the wording must be weakened.
