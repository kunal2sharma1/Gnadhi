# Product Manager — Master Book Architecture

## 1. Decision

The three independent architecture models converge strongly on a five-part transformation arc. The architecture will not be decade-by-decade and will not be a Gandhi biography.

## 2. Master Historical Spine

**Living Gandhi → unfinished final political project → political motive and assassination → loss of agency → assassination-specific 1948 shock/mourning/state construction → institutionalised posthumous Gandhi → competing inheritances/criticism/appropriation/counter-memory → selective contemporary test → what survived and changed.**

The assassination is the hinge, not a universal explanation for everything that follows.

## 3. Master Part Structure

### Part I — The Gandhi Who Was Still Acting
Establish Gandhi's final political agency and unfinished programme. Target: 3 chapters.

### Part II — The Decision to Remove Him
Explain Godse's political motive, conspiracy, organisations and responsibility boundaries. Target: 3 chapters.

### Part III — What the Assassination Set in Motion
Demonstrate the immediate assassination-specific transformation: loss of agency, shock, mourning, ritual, state response, and the first posthumous Gandhi. Target: 3 chapters.

### Part IV — Which Gandhi Survived?
Test substantive inheritance against symbolic survival through selected cases of canonisation, Gandhian movements, criticism, rejection, appropriation, neutralisation where demonstrable, and Godse counter-memory. Target: 5 chapters.

### Part V — The Contest Over Gandhi
Use a selective contemporary test and then synthesize what died, what survived, what changed and what cannot be established. Target: 2 chapters.

## 4. Master 16-Chapter Map

### Part I — The Gandhi Who Was Still Acting
1. **The Gandhi Who Was Still Acting** — establish Gandhi as a living political actor in 1947–48.
2. **The Last Gandhi: An Unfinished Political Project** — January fast, ₹55 crore context, communal politics, Lok Sevak Sangh draft.
3. **The Gandhi Godse Believed Had to Die** — Godse's political/ideological rationale, critically treated.

### Part II — The Decision to Remove Him
4. **The Conspiracy: From Belief to Action** — January 20 attempt, operational conspiracy, legal record.
5. **Ideology, Organisations, and the Limits of Responsibility** — RSS/Hindu Mahasabha/Savarkar questions with actor-specific attribution.
6. **The Killing** — 30 January assassination as the physical and political rupture; concise legal closure without letting trial detail dominate.

### Part III — What the Assassination Set in Motion
7. **The Political Meaning of Losing Gandhi** — loss of direct political agency; what death changes.
8. **The Shock: Mourning Becomes Politics** — immediate mourning, Nehru, Cabinet, Government resolutions, funeral and ritual.
9. **The First Gandhi After Gandhi** — early state/public construction, martyr framing, Gandhi/Godse paired memory, institutionalisation begins.

### Part IV — Which Gandhi Survived?
10. **The Gandhi the State Remembered** — canonisation and institutional memory; distinguish commemoration from substantive philosophy.
11. **The Gandhi Who Was Still Practised** — substantive Gandhian inheritance through movements/institutions; test political programme versus symbol.
12. **The Gandhis His Critics Rejected** — Ambedkarite and other serious criticisms; rejection is not destruction.
13. **The Gandhi Others Reclaimed** — selective inheritance and political appropriation; distinguish sincere partial inheritance from instrumental use.
14. **The Gandhi Countered by His Assassin** — Godse counter-memory and later glorification/rehabilitation as a distinct assassination-generated memory field.

### Part V — The Contest Over Gandhi
15. **Which Gandhi Exists Now?** — selective contemporary case studies: appropriation, criticism, counter-memory, neutralisation, or continuity only where proven.
16. **What Survived the Assassination?** — final synthesis: what died, what was fixed, what survived symbolically, what survived substantively, what changed, and what remains contested.

## 5. Structural Rules

- 1947–48 receives roughly one-third of the main text because every later claim depends on understanding the Gandhi who died and the immediate consequences of his murder.
- Earlier Gandhi biography enters only when needed to explain final-period positions, Godse's trajectory, or later inherited ideas.
- No full RSS, Hindu Mahasabha, Savarkar or Godse biographies.
- Post-1948 history is turning-point/case-study driven, not decade-driven.
- Every posthumous case must identify actor, version of Gandhi, transmission mechanism, competing interpretation, and political/institutional consequence.
- Criticism, rejection, appropriation, neutralisation, counter-memory and deliberate discrediting remain distinct categories.
- Modern continuity requires documented transmission; resemblance is not continuity.
- Contemporary material is a selective epilogue, not the organising frame.

## 6. Chapter Evidence Burden

Every chapter requires a clear question, primary-source base for load-bearing claims, serious scholarship, strongest counterargument, and a defined reason for existing.

The strongest documentary burden is on Chapters 1–9. Later chapters require case-specific evidence and must not carry more generality than their evidence supports.

## 7. Main Architectural Risk

The biggest risk is still **overextension**: the book could become a catalogue of things people have said or done with Gandhi. The architecture avoids this by requiring every later chapter to demonstrate a mechanism of posthumous meaning, not merely mention Gandhi.

## 8. Secondary Risk

The second major risk is treating the assassination as the cause of Gandhi's entire legacy. The structure explicitly limits the strongest assassination-specific causal claim to the immediate 1948 transformation and requires independent proof for later mechanisms.

## 9. Status

This is the master architecture entering Phase 2.3 stress testing. It remains provisional until the three independent stress tests are completed and reconciled.
