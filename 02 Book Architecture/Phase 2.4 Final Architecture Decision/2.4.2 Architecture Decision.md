## Central Causal Rule

The architecture does NOT claim:

> assassination caused Gandhi's entire later legacy.

It claims that the assassination created a distinctive immediate political condition in which:

- Gandhi lost direct political agency;
- violent death generated extraordinary political shock and mourning;
- the state and public actively interpreted the death;
- a first posthumous Gandhi was constructed;
- Gandhi's unfinished political endpoint became fixed;
- later actors inherited and contested different elements of that finite archive.

Every later development must have its own demonstrated mechanism.

## Required Mechanism Categories

Each posthumous case must explicitly identify which category it belongs to:

- canonisation;
- substantive inheritance/practice;
- criticism;
- rejection;
- selective appropriation;
- neutralisation, only if demonstrable;
- counter-memory;
- continuity, only if transmission is demonstrated;
- analogy;
- unrelated development.

## Evidence Rules

1. Primary sources carry load-bearing factual claims.
2. Godse's statements establish self-understanding, not complete causal truth.
3. Legal findings, commission findings and later scholarly interpretations must not be treated as interchangeable.
4. Organisational responsibility requires organisation-level evidence.
5. Symbolic presence does not equal substantive philosophical inheritance.
6. Criticism does not equal destruction.
7. Similarity does not equal continuity.
8. The present cannot be used as retroactive evidence of 1948 motives.

## Major Exclusions

The manuscript will not become:

- a full Gandhi biography;
- a full Godse biography;
- a complete history of the RSS or Hindu Mahasabha;
- a complete Savarkar history;
- a decade-by-decade survey of Gandhi's memory;
- a catalogue of contemporary Gandhi controversies;
- an argument that every later anti-Gandhi position belongs to one continuous ideology.

## Final Chapter Logic

Chapter 16 must classify survival rather than announce a single answer.

The conclusion should distinguish:

- Gandhi as living political actor — ended by assassination;
- direct political agency — ended;
- archive — preserved;
- national/moral symbolism — strongly survived;
- political programme — selectively and unevenly survived;
- interpretations — multiplied;
- contestation — persisted;
- direct 1948-to-present continuity — only where independently demonstrated.
