# Checkpoint 3: Chapter-Level Research Specification

## Objective

Convert the approved Checkpoint 2 architecture into a research-ready specification for every provisional chapter.

This checkpoint does **not** write manuscript chapters and does not attempt to complete all historical research. It defines exactly what each chapter must prove, what evidence it needs, what debates matter, what cases are eligible, what counterevidence must be included, and what conditions would justify changing or removing the chapter.

## Starting Baseline

Checkpoint 3 begins from:

- the completed Checkpoint 1 concept and evidence foundation;
- the revised Checkpoint 2 master architecture;
- the final architecture decision;
- the conditional status of Chapters 11, 13 and 15;
- the distinction between immediate assassination-specific effects and later independent mechanisms.

## Required Outcomes

1. A research specification for every approved/provisional chapter.
2. A load-bearing claim inventory for every chapter.
3. A primary-source requirement map.
4. A scholarly-literature requirement map.
5. A case-study shortlist where cases are necessary.
6. A counterevidence requirement for every major argument.
7. A chapter admission/removal/merge test.
8. A research-priority system.
9. A clear identification of chapters that remain conditional.

## Scope Limits

Do not turn this checkpoint into:

- manuscript drafting;
- broad unstructured Gandhi research;
- a complete bibliography;
- modern-politics research without a defined chapter purpose;
- premature final chapter word counts.

## Special Research Priorities

Priority must go to questions capable of changing the architecture:

1. pre-1948/post-1948 comparison of Gandhi's meaning and political position;
2. strongest evidence for assassination-specific immediate transformation;
3. substantive Gandhian inheritance suitable for Chapter 11;
4. documentary appropriation/inheritance suitable for Chapter 13;
5. one modern case capable of passing the strict continuity/inheritance test;
6. chapter-level evidence weakness or redundancy that could reduce the 16-chapter count.

## Completion Standard

Every chapter must eventually answer:

1. Why does this chapter exist?
2. What exactly must it prove?
3. What sources are required?
4. What competing interpretation must be addressed?
5. What case or evidence carries the argument?
6. What must the chapter avoid claiming?
7. What finding would justify merging, splitting, delaying or removing it?
