## Objective

Determine which cases can actually carry the later chapters and what evidence must be present before a chapter earns space in the manuscript.

Do not draft prose or perform broad research. Define selection tests and research targets.

## Required Analysis

For every provisional chapter identify:

- case-study requirement: none / optional / essential;
- candidate cases;
- mechanism each case must demonstrate;
- strongest alternative case;
- strongest counterevidence;
- evidence-risk level;
- admission test;
- merge/split trigger;
- remove trigger;
- research priority.

## Case Selection Rules

A case earns chapter-level space only if it:

- materially advances the central argument;
- demonstrates a distinct mechanism;
- has sufficient primary evidence;
- has serious scholarship or historiographical context;
- contains meaningful counterevidence;
- cannot be better handled in a shorter comparative section.

## Special Conditional Chapters

Develop strict admission tests for:

- Chapter 11: substantive Gandhian practice/inheritance;
- Chapter 13: selective appropriation/inheritance;
- Chapter 15: modern continuity or contemporary contestation.

A conditional chapter must be removable without collapsing the entire book argument.

## Counterevidence Architecture

For each major chapter identify evidence that could:

- weaken the claim;
- provide an alternative explanation;
- contradict the preferred interpretation;
- show the case is atypical;
- show a process existed before 1948;
- show a process was independent of assassination.

## Modern Case Test

Classify candidate modern cases as:

- continuity;
- inheritance;
- appropriation;
- counter-memory;
- recurrence;
- analogy;
- unrelated.

Define the minimum evidence required for each classification.
