# Checkpoint 3 — Phase Instructions

## Operating Principle

Checkpoint 3 is a specification checkpoint. Workers define research requirements; they do not write manuscript prose.

### Parallel means genuinely parallel

When three GPT workers run in parallel:

- all receive the same completed Checkpoint 2 baseline;
- each has a distinct responsibility;
- none reads another worker's new output;
- none waits for another worker;
- none makes its work conditional on another worker's conclusion.

### Sequential means genuinely sequential

Product Manager reconciliation happens only after all prerequisite worker outputs exist.

## Phase 3.1 — Independent Research Specification Models

### Worker 1 — Chapter Claim Architecture
Map the argument and load-bearing claims chapter by chapter.

### Worker 2 — Source and Scholarship Architecture
Map primary-source, archival and scholarly-literature requirements chapter by chapter.

### Worker 3 — Case Study, Counterevidence and Admission Architecture
Map cases, counterarguments, exclusions and chapter admission/removal criteria.

## Phase 3.2 — Product Manager Reconciliation

Compare the three independent models and produce one master chapter research specification.

## Phase 3.3 — Priority and Dependency Review

Identify:

- highest-value research questions;
- chapter dependencies;
- conditional chapters;
- research that can run in parallel;
- research that must wait for earlier findings.

## Phase 3.4 — Research Specification Stress Test

Three GPT workers independently attack the master specification:

- Worker 1: argument/claim integrity;
- Worker 2: source sufficiency and scholarly coverage;
- Worker 3: case-study viability, counterevidence and chapter admission.

## Phase 3.5 — Final Research Specification Lock

Product Manager reconciles the stress tests and locks the chapter-by-chapter research specifications.

## Phase 3.6 — Completion Review

Create:

- completion audit;
- next checkpoint design brief;
- formal Checkpoint 3 completion review.

## Quality Rule

A chapter is not research-ready unless its specification makes clear:

1. why it exists;
2. what it must prove;
3. what evidence can prove it;
4. what evidence could defeat it;
5. what competing interpretation must be handled;
6. what would justify cutting or restructuring it.
